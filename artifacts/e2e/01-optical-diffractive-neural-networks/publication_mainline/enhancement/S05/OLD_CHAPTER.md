Diffractive deep neural networks are widely adopted for their ultra-low latency, low power consumption, and highly parallel optical computing capability, though hardware implementation robustness remains a critical consideration [[claim:S05-P128]]. These fundamental performance characteristics underpin the growing body of work applying diffractive architectures to real-world tasks spanning imaging, classification, segmentation, and pattern recognition. The applications range from single-function diffractive lenses optimized for specific wavelengths to general-purpose metasurface-based processors capable of executing multiple distinct inference tasks without retraining or hardware modifications.

Image classification represents the most mature application domain for ODNNs, with experimentally validated systems achieving high accuracy on standard benchmarks. Diffractive deep neural networks (D2NNs) have been experimentally demonstrated for image classification tasks using metasurface-based architectures, achieving performance competitive with electronic baselines in specific single-function settings [[claim:S05-P033]]. Early demonstrations included handwritten digit classification at visible wavelengths, where five-layer D2NNs with one million neurons per layer achieved blind classification accuracy of approximately 91.57% on held-out test sets comprising both unchanged and deformed MNIST digits, without requiring electronic computation during inference [[claim:S05-P065]]. More recent work has extended these results to multi-class image classification tasks on standard datasets such as MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, with specific architectures achieving high accuracy through end-to-end joint optimization of phase profiles and computational decoders [[claim:S05-P001]].

Beyond conventional digit and object recognition, diffractive networks have been adapted for specialized classification tasks including alphabetic recognition, medical diagnostics, and structured-light processing. Multi-layer diffractive optical neural networks (D2NNs) have been demonstrated for alphabetic image recognition, leveraging parallel pixel-wise computation to achieve inference at the speed of light [[claim:S05-P105]]. In the medical imaging domain, diffractive neural networks can perform all-optical classification of diagnostic data, such as identifying COVID-19 from chest X-ray images, demonstrating the viability of ODNNs for specific clinical applications [[claim:S05-P073]]. Specialized configurations utilizing orbital angular momentum (OAM) encoding can perform all-optical classification and spectrum analysis on MNIST datasets using a single output detector, demonstrating a novel approach for structured light processing [[claim:S05-P025]]. Image classification implementations span multiple wavelength regimes, including all-optical systems using nonlinear activation mechanisms and visible-light metasurface-based multiplexed systems capable of complex object recognition [[claim:S05-P057]].

A distinguishing advantage of diffractive architectures is their capacity for hardware-level multiplexing, enabling a single physical device to execute multiple distinct inference tasks simultaneously. Polarization multiplexing in a single diffractive network enables all-optical implementation of multiple complex-valued arbitrary linear transformations simultaneously at the same output field of view, allowing different machine vision tasks such as detection, classification, and localization of objects to be programmed into different input/output polarization states [[claim:S05-P002]]. Metasurface-empowered diffractive neural networks support multi-task learning through hardware-software co-design, enabling a single physical device to execute multiple distinct inference tasks such as classification and segmentation simultaneously [[claim:S05-P045]]. Multifunctional diffractive processors enable single-hardware multi-task learning, allowing a single physical device to perform multiple distinct optical inference tasks without retraining or hardware changes [[claim:S05-P131]]. This multiplexing extends beyond polarization; orbital angular momentum (OAM) mode multiplexing enables the reconstruction of multiple holograms within a single diffractive optical neural network, expanding information capacity through compatibility with wavelength and polarization dimensions [[claim:S05-P113]].

In computational imaging, diffractive elements have enabled achromatic broadband operation, extended depth of field, and superresolution capabilities that challenge conventional optical design constraints. Monolithic diffractive optical elements can achieve achromatic imaging across an extended field of view by relaxing the fundamental trade-offs between aperture size, numerical aperture, and operational bandwidth [[claim:S05-P017]]. Hybrid diffractive optics designs using hardware-in-the-loop methodologies enable achromatic extended-depth-of-field imaging, representing a mature application of diffractive elements in computational imaging [[claim:S05-P055]]. Diffractive neural networks have been applied to superresolution imaging by overcoming the Abbe-Rayleigh diffraction limit through superoscillatory designs, enabling near-field microscopic imaging capabilities that surpass traditional optical equipment constraints [[claim:S05-P089]]. Compressive sensing systems combined with machine learning algorithms facilitate image analysis by leveraging large datasets of labeled data available through increased use of machine learning, with task-specific optical architectures demonstrated for classifying datasets like MNIST [[claim:S05-P007]].

Integrated and on-chip implementations, along with specialized applications such as all-optical decryption and coded ptychography, demonstrate the expanding scope of diffractive processing. Two-dimensional photonic crystal diffractive optical neural networks (PhC-DONNs) realize on-chip optical parallel classification schemes with multi-wavelength parallel processing capabilities [[claim:S05-P004]]. Digitized phase-change material heterostacks enable transmissive diffractive optical neural networks with reconfigurability, supporting high-throughput and energy-efficient machine learning processing for applications like computer vision [[claim:S05-P030]]. Physics-informed diffractive neural networks can approximate inverse transmission matrices to enable real-time decryption of speckle ciphertexts in all-optical high-speed schemes [[claim:S05-P003]]. Parallel coded ptychography systems utilizing diffractive principles demonstrate turnkey high-throughput optical imaging capabilities to address scaling complexity obstacles in array microscopy for biomedical applications [[claim:S05-P016]].

Despite these advances, practical deployment faces persistent challenges including simulation-to-reality gaps, environmental sensitivity, and the need for adaptive compensation strategies. On-chip diffractive optical neural networks based on integrated rewritable metasurfaces face a simulation-to-reality gap that limits their immediate deployment viability compared to electronic baselines [[claim:S05-P049]]. Photonic neural networks leverage nonlinear behavior advantageous for implementing activation functions, though resonators remain vulnerable to environmental factors like temperature fluctuations and mechanical vibrations, which can adversely impact their performance [[claim:S05-P006]]. Aberration-adaptive learning stabilizes diffractive deep neural network performance by adapting the model to compensate for wavefront aberrations, enabling robust operation in non-ideal optical conditions [[claim:S05-P097]]. Hybrid optical-electronic convolutional neural networks utilize optimized diffractive optics to handle color-sensitive kernels, addressing limitations of monochromatic imaging in natural environments [[claim:S05-P005]].

Diffractive deep neural networks (D2NNs) have demonstrated orders-of-magnitude improvements in inference speed and energy efficiency compared to electronic counterparts, establishing their viability for high-speed, low-power optical computing applications [[claim:S05-P129]]. Optical neural networks leveraging photonic hardware acceleration offer advantages in ultra-high bandwidth, high calculation speed, and high parallelism compared to current electronic computing devices [[claim:S05-P059]]. The maturity of ODNN applications varies significantly across domains: classification and single-function imaging approaches operational readiness, while multi-task integration, on-chip deployment, and robust operation in uncontrolled environments remain active research frontiers. As fabrication techniques advance and hybrid electro-optical architectures mature, diffractive neural networks are positioned to complement electronic systems in applications where speed, energy efficiency, and parallelism are paramount.
