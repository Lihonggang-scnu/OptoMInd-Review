The phase modulation imparted by a fixed physical diffractive structure scales with wavelength, causing the point spread function and transfer function to shift as illumination wavelength varies [REF:a90fa0c5db2d3fae860863e1af0d7f6d62a8a7c4][REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63][REF:1a72dd2ce2d2bc166146ac27110cac04b58eb74d][REF:cdb2c928e087f4cd2b807836fd131089bf81a711]. Diffractive optical neural networks (DONNs) are therefore inherently dispersive because the phase profile of a diffractive layer produces wavelength-dependent transfer functions, causing chromatic degradation of computational accuracy as the input wavelength deviates from the training wavelength [REF:3e638d8c69aa2b7403f346cb3babaa20f182b5c2][REF:5c7e5248d9eb7f373f10277410bf8506160907ea][REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63][REF:1a72dd2ce2d2bc166146ac27110cac04b58eb74d][REF:cdb2c928e087f4cd2b807836fd131089bf81a711]. This fundamental property distinguishes diffractive layers from digital neural network weights, which are wavelength-independent; in the optical domain, a phase profile optimized for one wavelength yields a materially different transfer function at another. Diffractive deep neural networks (D2NNs) share this characteristic: their transfer function and computational accuracy vary with wavelength because the phase profile is optimized for a specific operating wavelength [REF:614a70af75ba590cc98dfc4c8aeec02322b5906f][REF:916246d3fb00fde0d8f1223f5343447e6d964168][REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63][REF:1a72dd2ce2d2bc166146ac27110cac04b58eb74d][REF:cdb2c928e087f4cd2b807836fd131089bf81a711].

The practical consequence of this dispersion is measurable performance degradation under off-specification illumination. Diffractive optical neural networks exhibit inherent chromatic dispersion where the point spread function and transfer function shift with wavelength, causing performance degradation when operating outside the training wavelength [REF:b06d7f7742ed94078f512011968ae123e576b59f]. Networks trained for single-wavelength operation show significant performance decline when illuminated by broadband or shifted wavelengths due to chromatic dispersion altering the phase transfer function [REF:34d7ba5c285f38e34ad058f6b2f4e7e55c2fc224]. Diffractive optical neural networks (ODNNs) are inherently dispersive because the phase profile of a diffractive layer is wavelength-dependent, causing the transfer function to change with wavelength and limiting broadband operation unless specific mitigation strategies are employed [REF:967d18a887694471d29d04787466874a779201ff][REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63][REF:1a72dd2ce2d2bc166146ac27110cac04b58eb74d][REF:cdb2c928e087f4cd2b807836fd131089bf81a711]. Diffractive neural networks similarly exhibit inherent chromatic dispersion where the point spread function and transfer function shift with wavelength, limiting broadband computational accuracy unless targeted compensation methods are applied [REF:e9eb5f09e2404cc46986e623c8ef093ffddadeec][REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63][REF:1a72dd2ce2d2bc166146ac27110cac04b58eb74d][REF:cdb2c928e087f4cd2b807836fd131089bf81a711].

To address these constraints, several mitigation strategies have emerged. Hybrid diffractive optics designs employing hardware-in-the-loop optimization achieve achromatic extended depth-of-field imaging, mitigating chromatic dispersion through iterative alignment and phase modulation adjustments [REF:00af565d66563cb83347d304c37313cc2dfa0902][REF:c2b2d0a1d0d15507e17d2dbbb871ee4d82e2c827][REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63][REF:1a72dd2ce2d2bc166146ac27110cac04b58eb74d][REF:cdb2c928e087f4cd2b807836fd131089bf81a711]. Achromatic metalens designs utilizing bilayer architectures or multi-layer three-dimensional-printed structures can significantly expand the operational bandwidth of diffractive elements compared to single-layer counterparts [REF:doi:10.29026/oea.2021.200008][REF:doi:10.1126/sciadv.adj9262][REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63][REF:1a72dd2ce2d2bc166146ac27110cac04b58eb74d][REF:cdb2c928e087f4cd2b807836fd131089bf81a711]. Multi-layer thin film structures and dual-scale nanostructures can be optimized using multi-wavelength weighted strategies to manage dispersion and improve anti-reflection properties across bands, extending the full width at half maximum of the spectrum substantially beyond traditional single-wavelength coatings [REF:c977f316916c59a12a847854a4eef6f49381b590]. These approaches share a common principle: rather than treating dispersion as an unavoidable artifact, they incorporate wavelength diversity explicitly into the design optimization loop.

The same wavelength dependence that constrains broadband operation also enables powerful multiplexing capabilities. A multifunctional diffractive processor with learned structured phases enables optical multi-task learning by exploiting the wavelength-dependent properties of the diffractive layers [REF:4ec17510a4fcc9d4756694cc4efa9bba74b83503][REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63][REF:1a72dd2ce2d2bc166146ac27110cac04b58eb74d][REF:cdb2c928e087f4cd2b807836fd131089bf81a711]. Multi-wavelength optical diffractive networks support multitask learning by assigning distinct computational functions to different spectral bands, effectively multiplexing independent operations within a single static phase profile [REF:a8191fd687585196d26f1c0758b618c56e475696][REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63][REF:1a72dd2ce2d2bc166146ac27110cac04b58eb74d][REF:cdb2c928e087f4cd2b807836fd131089bf81a711]. Wavelength multiplexing enables a single diffractive optical element to perform multiple independent computational tasks simultaneously by encoding distinct functions for different spectral bands [REF:438f30f7d53e93e1f20e50ee62f1029f509ed4e5][REF:40c350c03d9595a626dff473898a54a2bdfc9867][REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63][REF:1a72dd2ce2d2bc166146ac27110cac04b58eb74d][REF:cdb2c928e087f4cd2b807836fd131089bf81a711]. In each case, the chromatic sensitivity that would otherwise degrade performance becomes the enabling mechanism for functional multiplexing.

Additional degrees of freedom further expand multiplexing capacity. Polarization multiplexing allows a single diffractive network to implement multiple complex-valued linear transformations concurrently at the same output field of view, requiring only the addition of linear polarizers without changing the diffractive architecture [REF:04c16f79232a1dba281da8a2626e1933bcb10641]. However, these gains face practical limits: at shorter wavelengths and smaller differences in input wavelengths, fabrication variability may play a key role in experimental model performance, and single- and two-layer DNNs designed to classify multiple wavelengths in parallel performed poorly, indicating that higher layer counts will be necessary for robust multi-wavelength classification [REF:34d7ba5c285f38e34ad058f6b2f4e7e55c2fc224].

The design of diffractive layers involves optimizing complex-valued transmission coefficients for specific statistical tasks, which inherently ties the network's functionality to the illumination spectrum used during training [REF:e21c4bb77ec29f0e6b14e6a98ab872b0dfedd2f5][REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63][REF:1a72dd2ce2d2bc166146ac27110cac04b58eb74d][REF:cdb2c928e087f4cd2b807836fd131089bf81a711]. Diffractive neural networks can exploit optical degrees of freedom, such as wavelength multiplexing, to implement multiple independent computational functions within a single physical structure [REF:e265d8653dabb3fa0068ab956b084f8f252bdbcf][REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63][REF:1a72dd2ce2d2bc166146ac27110cac04b58eb74d][REF:cdb2c928e087f4cd2b807836fd131089bf81a711]. Integrated diffractive photonic computing units offer ultra-high bandwidth and high parallelism compared to electronic devices, providing a hardware motivation for exploring wavelength-dependent optical computation despite its constraints [REF:585c02f3ea677e72535f4ea2af006230e5892445][REF:c3878ee93c5882c47cf566762644e36fe997e521][REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63][REF:1a72dd2ce2d2bc166146ac27110cac04b58eb74d][REF:cdb2c928e087f4cd2b807836fd131089bf81a711]. The central challenge remains balancing the suppression of unwanted chromatic effects against the deliberate exploitation of wavelength selectivity—a tension that defines the emerging design methodology for wavelength-aware diffractive computing systems.
