The diffractive neural network (DNN) field is currently navigating a period of productive but contentious self-examination. At the heart of the controversy lies a fundamental question: do diffractive deep neural networks (D2NNs) offer genuine computational advantages over conventional optical processing, or are they primarily engineering exercises demonstrating known optical phenomena with added complexity? [[claim:S06-P033]] This debate is not merely semantic; it shapes funding priorities, research direction, and the community's assessment of what constitutes meaningful progress. Critics point out that many reported performance gaps depend heavily on specific benchmark datasets and simulation conditions rather than fundamental physical limits, suggesting the field lacks consensus on whether these systems deliver transformative capability or simply elegant demonstrations of wave optics. [[claim:S06-P110]]

A persistent and well-documented concern is that idealized simulation models systematically overestimate practical efficacy. Researchers rely on Fourier optics and scalar diffraction models that often neglect material dispersion, fabrication-induced surface roughness, and misalignment errors—factors that significantly degrade performance in physical prototypes. [[claim:S06-P105]] These models idealize free-space propagation and material interactions, introducing discrepancies between simulated and actual behavior. [[claim:S06-P027]] The need for Zernike correction and multi-objective optimization in physical implementations further underscores how real-world aberrations and dispersion complicate the clean picture presented by simulations. [[claim:S06-P015]] This reliance on idealized in-silico training creates an additional layer of difficulty: most D2NN frameworks assume nonlinear activation functions like ReLU during training, yet physical diffractive layers lack native nonlinearity, necessitating either hybrid electronic assistance or complex fabrication approximations. [[claim:S06-P097]] Consequently, the claimed computational advantages over conventional electronic systems may be overstated due to favorable simulation assumptions that ignore scattering losses, material absorption, and manufacturing tolerances—all of which significantly impact real-world energy efficiency and throughput. [[claim:S06-P108]] The resulting simulation-to-reality gap necessitates more rigorous experimental validation before definitive claims about computational superiority can be substantiated. [[claim:S06-P045]]

The dominant training paradigm itself faces scrutiny. End-to-end differentiable training through the diffraction integral enables high-throughput all-optical processing, but this approach encounters a fundamental gradient chain break when discrete device mappings are introduced. [[claim:S06-P017]] The physical discretization required for fabrication breaks the smooth differentiability assumed by backpropagation algorithms, limiting the fidelity with which theoretically optimal weight configurations can be realized in hardware. [[claim:S06-P041]] This limitation has prompted proposals for alternative training paradigms. Some researchers advocate physics-aware optimization and sparse coding as approaches that could improve performance-per-parameter ratios and enhance robustness against physical imperfections. [[claim:S06-P058]] Whether end-to-end differentiable learning remains the optimal paradigm—or should be supplemented or replaced by methods incorporating explicit physical priors—remains an active area of investigation.

The architectural philosophy of the field reveals another fault line. On one side stand single-function optimized designs, such as chromatic-aberration-corrected lenses, that deliver superior performance for narrowly defined tasks but offer no flexibility beyond their intended purpose. [[claim:S06-P100]] On the other are general-purpose trainable architectures that promise broad applicability but face significant trade-offs between flexibility and efficiency. Their multi-layer designs demand complex fabrication processes and precise alignment, whereas single-function optimized designs offer simpler implementation at the cost of adaptability. [[claim:S06-P107]] The requirement for mechanical reconfiguration or distinct hardware per task in earlier designs created a complexity overhead that challenged the computational advantage of single-function optimized architectures compared to general-purpose trainable systems. [[claim:S06-P025]] This tension between maximizing task-specific performance and maintaining versatility reflects a deeper philosophical disagreement about what diffractive neural networks should ultimately achieve. [[claim:S06-P133]]

Real-world deployment confronts formidable engineering obstacles. Photonic neural network resonators exhibit nonlinear behaviors useful for optical activation functions, but their performance is fundamentally limited by environmental sensitivity to temperature fluctuations and mechanical vibrations. [[claim:S06-P001]] Fabrication variability introduces significant uncertainty in broadband diffractive neural network performance, particularly at shorter wavelengths, suggesting that simulation-based advantages may not fully translate to physical hardware without advanced manufacturing control. [[claim:S06-P011]] Real-world deployment also requires robust processing of incoherent and partially coherent light, whereas most theoretical frameworks assume idealized quasimonochromatic illumination conditions. [[claim:S06-P019]] Integration with existing semiconductor processes remains a key bottleneck, with current demonstrations largely confined to proof-of-concept setups rather than scalable, manufacturable platforms compatible with standard CMOS lines. [[claim:S06-P064]] Hardware implementability challenges, including fixed architecture limitations and alignment sensitivity, pose significant barriers to deploying general-purpose trainable diffractive neural networks at scale. [[claim:S06-P130]]

Some researchers argue that minimalist configurations offer a compelling alternative to complex trainable networks. Minimalist diffractive neural networks with straightforward optical configurations have demonstrated robustness and scalability in image classification tasks, suggesting that elaborate trainable architectures may not always be necessary for specific applications. [[claim:S06-P081]] All-optical diffractive neural networks can achieve inference speeds and energy efficiencies surpassing conventional electronic CNNs, though this advantage is contingent on the task being strictly optical classification where digital data conversion latency is avoided. [[claim:S06-P009]] However, even promising approaches face constraints: training different tasks on separate spatial regions of a single diffractive layer is limited by physical area, restricting simultaneously supported fixed functions to approximately two or three. [[claim:S06-P089]]

Several critical questions remain unresolved. A significant portion of the reported performance gap between ODNNs and electronic benchmarks may reflect favorable simulation assumptions rather than physical reality, particularly regarding wavelength dependence, alignment tolerances, and material losses. [[claim:S06-P059]] The simulation-to-reality performance gap exists across multiple platform types, from bulk free-space systems to on-chip implementations, where idealized models may overestimate practical efficacy. [[claim:S06-P049]] While proponents cite ultra-high bandwidth and parallelism for matrix-vector multiplication as evidence of genuine computational advantage, critics argue that reported efficiency gains are often offset by substantial training costs and fabrication complexities. [[claim:S06-P057]] The integration of multiple optical functionalities introduces inherent complexity, often requiring complex optical encoding schemes, multi-step measurements, or intricate reconstruction algorithms that complicate practical deployment. [[claim:S06-P065]] Diffractive neural networks trained through end-to-end differentiable methods have shown the ability to reconstruct objects through unknown random diffusers without retraining, suggesting potential for broader applicability in scattering environments. [[claim:S06-P073]] Meanwhile, optoelectronic frameworks for lensless imaging raise a conceptual question: do diffractive elements themselves perform computation, or merely act as passive encoders for downstream algorithms? [[claim:S06-P103]] And while diffractive optical neural networks offer passive energy efficiency and low-latency inference through photonic computation, achieving fully programmable all-optical reconfigurability remains a significant engineering challenge compared to static designs. [[claim:S06-P121]]
