The transition from laboratory diffractive neural network prototypes to deployable optical AI systems confronts an interconnected set of engineering and physical bottlenecks that must be resolved before the field can mature beyond proof-of-concept demonstrations. Early all-optical diffractive deep neural networks demonstrated ultra-low latency and high parallelism but suffered from fixed hardware architectures that prevented post-fabrication reconfigurability [[claim:S07-P129]]. These foundational limitations underscore the need for systematic analysis of which obstacles are fundamentally physical versus solvable through engineering innovation.

The most pervasive engineering challenge lies in the inherent reconfigurability limit of static diffractive hardware, which constrains practical deployment to single-task or narrowly multi-function devices. Static diffractive neural networks are fundamentally limited in reconfigurability, requiring either mechanical movement of optical elements or the fabrication of separate hardware instances for each task, which hinders scalable multi-task deployment [[claim:S07-P025]]. Conventional static diffractive neural networks suffer from limited reconfigurability due to their write-once fabrication nature, necessitating hybrid electro-optical co-design or active materials to enable dynamic adaptability for real-world deployment [[claim:S07-P073]]. Current diffractive neural network research is further constrained by area limitations when training different tasks on separate regions of the diffractive layer, restricting the number of fixed functions to only two or three per device and highlighting a key engineering challenge for scalable multi-function optical AI [[claim:S07-P084]]. These constraints collectively motivate architectural paradigms that decouple the learned diffractive function from the need for complete hardware redesign.

Fabrication-induced phase errors and simulation-to-reality gaps impose additional constraints that become increasingly severe at shorter wavelengths and with deeper network architectures. Diffractive neural networks can achieve robust classification through unknown random diffusers using single-wavelength inference, demonstrating a pathway toward deployment in complex scattering environments where electronic preprocessing is impractical, yet fabrication variability significantly impacts performance at shorter wavelengths and narrow spectral bandwidths, necessitating fabrication-aware training or post-fabrication correction strategies to maintain accuracy [[claim:S07-P010]][[claim:S07-P009]]. Fabrication-induced surface roughness introduces phase errors in diffractive optical neural networks, necessitating physics-aware optimization strategies to maintain inference accuracy against manufacturing tolerances [[claim:S07-P061]]. Fabrication-induced phase errors in dielectric metasurfaces remain a critical bottleneck for scalable optical AI, requiring fabrication-aware training or post-fabrication correction strategies to achieve high-fidelity wavefront control [[claim:S07-P090]]. Fabrication-induced phase errors and interlayer reflections impose fundamental limits on the accuracy of polychromatic and multi-layer diffractive designs, necessitating fabrication-aware training protocols [[claim:S07-P123]]. Simulation-to-reality gaps in diffractive neural networks, particularly for on-chip implementations, represent a critical engineering bottleneck that requires fabrication-aware training and hardware-in-the-loop optimization to bridge [[claim:S07-P049]].

Addressing these fabrication and coherence challenges requires physics-aware training paradigms that explicitly account for device nonuniformity, wavefront aberrations, and partial coherence conditions. Directly adding discrete mapping from device to diffractive optical neural network systems breaks the gradient chain in backpropagation, necessitating physics-aware differentiable discrete codesign to enable effective training [[claim:S07-P018]]. Aberration-adaptive learning stabilizes diffractive deep neural network performance by adapting the model to compensate for wavefront aberrations, mitigating a key physical bottleneck in optical deployment [[claim:S07-P098]]. Real-world deployment of diffractive optical neural networks requires processing incoherent and partially coherent light, as current models often assume quasimonochromatic illumination which limits applicability to specific lighting conditions [[claim:S07-P019]]. These methodological advances point toward training frameworks that close the gap between idealized numerical emulation and the noisy, nonuniform reality of optical hardware.

Hybrid electro-optical architectures emerge as the most promising near-term trajectory, combining the parallelism and energy efficiency of passive diffractive layers with the reconfigurability of electronic control. Hybrid electro-optical architectures that integrate static diffractive layers for high-throughput, low-latency feature extraction with reconfigurable electronic layers for nonlinear activation and logic can mitigate the limited reconfigurability of all-optical hardware while preserving energy efficiency gains [[claim:S07-P033]]. Hybrid electro-optical architectures that integrate programmable diffractive deep neural networks with digital-coding metasurfaces can leverage the parallelism of optical propagation while retaining the flexibility of electronic control, addressing the reconfigurability bottleneck of static hardware [[claim:S07-P041]]. Hybrid electro-optical architectures can mitigate the limited reconfigurability of static diffractive neural networks by using electronic layers for dynamic weight updates while preserving optical inference speed, addressing a key engineering bottleneck identified in prior sections [[claim:S07-P089]]. Hybrid electro-optical architectures offer a viable near-term trajectory by leveraging optical inference speed for specific tasks while retaining electronic flexibility for reconfigurability and training updates [[claim:S07-P122]]. All-optical diffractive classifiers provide distinct advantages in real-time operation compared to conventional approaches, though they currently achieve lower raw accuracy, supporting hybrid electro-optical architectures that leverage optical speed for specific sub-tasks [[claim:S07-P011]].

Beyond hybrid co-design, tunable materials and alternative all-optical strategies offer complementary pathways toward dynamic functionality without sacrificing the core advantages of optical propagation. The integration of tunable nonlinear activation functions using materials like tungsten trioxide films allows adaptive diffractive deep neural networks to overcome the static nonlinearity bottleneck, enabling dynamic reconfigurability essential for hybrid electro-optical co-design [[claim:S07-P083]]. Hybrid electro-optical approaches, such as online learning techniques for magneto-optical systems, allow dynamic reconfigurability of diffractive layers, addressing the bottleneck of static hardware limitations in purely passive ODNNs [[claim:S07-P062]]. Cascade diffractive architectures can circumvent the need for nonlinear optical activations by utilizing modulation-based operations, addressing a fundamental limitation in all-optical neural network design [[claim:S07-P099]]. Data-driven inverse design frameworks using differentiable neural simulators enable the optimization of metasurface geometries for specific spectral imaging tasks, offering a pathway to overcome the narrow operational bandwidth of single-function diffractive elements [[claim:S07-P092]]. Multi-element metasurface systems and achromatic designs address the wavelength-dependent performance limitations of diffractive optics, enabling broader operational bandwidths required for practical hyperspectral and broadband imaging applications [[claim:S07-P093]]. Polarization-multiplexed metasurface-based all-optical neural networks can perform complex object recognition tasks, demonstrating that multi-functional optical processing is feasible within a single static diffractive element [[claim:S07-P065]].

Standardized benchmarking protocols remain underdeveloped, yet specific inference tasks already demonstrate the viability of minimalist optical configurations despite remaining limitations. Standardized benchmarking datasets for diffractive neural networks currently face limitations such as being restricted to two-dimensional spatial imaging, indicating an open gap for hyperspectral or volumetric data validation required for broader deployment [[claim:S07-P087]]. Diffractive deep neural networks can be deployed for specific inference tasks such as holographic data storage and image classification, demonstrating that minimalist optical configurations with efficient computational capabilities are viable near-term applications despite limited reconfigurability [[claim:S07-P081]]. Transfer learning and multifunctional processor designs represent near-term engineering solutions to overcome the rigidity of static diffractive hardware, allowing reuse of pre-trained layers or multi-task capabilities without full re-fabrication [[claim:S07-P132]]. Fully programmable all-optical diffractive neural networks remain experimentally unachieved due to the physical difficulty of integrating dynamic, low-loss phase modulation directly into dielectric metasurfaces at scale [[claim:S07-P121]]. Whether this gap can ever be closed, or whether hybrid electro-optical co-design will remain the dominant paradigm for scalable optical AI, represents one of the field's most consequential open questions.
