Photonic integrated circuits (PICs) offer superior energy efficiency and scalability compared to conventional electronic computing systems, particularly for solving combinatorial optimization problems [REF:doi:10.3390/nano14080697][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. The ability to perform linear algebra operations at the speed of light with minimal Joule heating makes photonic architectures especially attractive for large-scale matrix computations underlying neural network inference and scientific simulation. However, realizing these advantages at chip scale requires careful navigation of material property constraints, device-level loss mechanisms, and fabrication realities that become increasingly consequential as component counts grow from dozens to thousands on a single die.

Silicon photonics remains the dominant platform for dense, CMOS-compatible photonic computing due to its mature fabrication ecosystem and the extensive process design kits available through foundry services [REF:7182e044e8f1d2714a81f386c6cfd0b8c3aeb492][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Silicon-on-insulator substrates enable sub-micron waveguide dimensions and tight bending radii, supporting integration densities unattainable on most alternative platforms. Yet this maturity comes at a cost: silicon lacks a linear electro-optic effect, meaning that modulation must rely on either carrier-depletion mechanisms or thermo-optic phase shifters, both of which introduce significant power consumption and bandwidth limitations. Two-photon absorption losses at high optical intensities further constrain the usable power budget in densely packed circuits [REF:7182e044e8f1d2714a81f386c6cfd0b8c3aeb492][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Whether silicon-on-insulator photonics can fully address the power and bandwidth requirements of scalable photonic computing given these intrinsic material limitations remains an area of active investigation [REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1].

Thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) and silicon nitride (Si₃N₄) present compelling alternatives with superior intrinsic material properties. TFLN offers a strong Pockels electro-optic coefficient enabling high-speed, low-voltage modulation with propagation losses competitive with or lower than silicon photonics, making it well-suited for energy-efficient photonic computing applications [REF:d8e3a88b8ae7dde16b03c595d6ec21383e205c78][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Its principal limitation lies in integration density: the thicker film thickness required for effective confinement restricts minimum bend radii relative to silicon. Silicon nitride, meanwhile, exhibits a nonlinear susceptibility of approximately 2.5×10⁻¹⁹ m²/W at 1.55 μm, enabling efficient four-wave mixing processes suitable for applications such as Kerr microcomb generation [REF:d8e3a88b8ae7dde16b03c595d6ec21383e205c78][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Si₃N₄ also benefits from ultra-low propagation loss in the visible and near-infrared spectral windows, though its weak electro-optic response similarly necessitates auxiliary modulation schemes.

The choice of primary photonic building block for implementing matrix-vector multiplication (MVM)—the core linear operation in photonic neural networks—involves fundamental architectural trade-offs. Mach-Zehnder interferometer (MZI) arrays provide deterministic, broadband control over amplitude and phase but require one thermo-optic or electro-optic phase shifter per arm, leading to substantial power dissipation in large matrices [REF:f7bd07322c2336c8e12a77f9c4303d2a8768892a][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Micro-ring resonators offer a smaller footprint per tunable element and can be cascaded more compactly, but they suffer from narrow operating bandwidths, sensitivity to fabrication variations, and potential crosstalk between adjacent resonators [REF:f7bd07322c2336c8e12a77f9c4303d2a8768892a][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Diffractive optical neural networks (DONNs) implemented on silicon-on-insulator substrates achieve all-optical, passive computation with high throughput and low latency, though they face complex optical constraints related to layer alignment and fixed weight programming. Photonic resonators used in DONNs exhibit nonlinear behavior advantageous for implementing activation functions in neural networks, but they are vulnerable to environmental factors such as temperature fluctuations and mechanical vibrations that can degrade computational accuracy [REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7]. On-chip dielectric metasurfaces offer a unique scheme to control the degrees of freedom of the on-chip light field and are compatible with standard CMOS fabrication processes, positioning them as another candidate for scalable photonic computing integration [REF:ed14198e3579a78f1802b519db669f0ec2185d51][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. The selection among free-space diffraction systems, MZI arrays, and micro-ring resonators involves trade-offs in scalability, power consumption, and programmability, with integrated approaches generally favored for chip-scale photonic computing despite their higher insertion losses [REF:f7bd07322c2336c8e12a77f9c4303d2a8768892a][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1].

At scale, several physical error mechanisms converge to limit the achievable fidelity of large photonic computing circuits. Fabrication variations such as finite precision in waveguide width introduce fundamental phase variations that corrupt matrix-vector multiplication operations in coherent nanophotonic neural networks, with sensitivity scaling directly with nominal device dimensions [REF:955db7b00b8b503d46c2749d399075e11f40c61b][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Inverse design of nanophotonic devices is further constrained by fabrication tolerances—specifically minimum feature sizes and length-scale constraints—which limit the accessible design space compared to unconstrained optimization [REF:77dd2a80bc5f7d1504a411a519cae5c5e5074099][REF:72104901d05262d178fce1a028164d85f5fba671][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Compounding these physical errors, the primary challenge in scalable photonic circuit design lies in the oversimplification of transfer matrices by heuristic methods, which leads to unimplementable responses and subsequent performance degradation compared to rigorous simulation-based approaches [REF:c9e5de816ca2356737e7f23532658ef28e6b2738]. The objective functions in photonic inverse design are often highly irregular and oscillatory due to cyclic interference effects, creating local minima that challenge standard gradient-based optimizers [REF:89bb23461718c3625956046c2a901ce408c8230b][REF:cea9e6112f363e19aa754d2681622ab07650c71c][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1].

Physics-informed machine learning and inverse design methodologies are emerging as critical enablers for managing the complexity of wafer-scale photonic circuits under realistic fabrication constraints. Deep learning and neural operators are increasingly used to simulate and optimize photonic devices, enabling faster design cycles for complex integrated circuits compared to traditional finite-difference frequency domain methods [REF:15ccb57280963ed7debeebb95d5a8f2418fb55d3][REF:7182e044e8f1d2714a81f386c6cfd0b8c3aeb492][REF:identity-fallback:e49491a3a2a5b595][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Physics-informed neural operators and adjoint-based inverse design methods address the computational bottlenecks associated with scaling device counts in photonic computing architectures [REF:d45611ec6c8ef616676ecbb1851bf6a398a23967][REF:717a7acde176d0cfa323ca21c76df50c954e57bc][REF:identity-fallback:8b60e3709a8988ac][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Deep neural networks serve as fast surrogate models to approximate the optical response of nanostructures, enabling rapid inverse design and optimization of integrated photonic devices such as power splitters [REF:8535f8effb032edcbbe608d47dcffb0e4fc0b7d8][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Machine learning algorithms, including deep convolutional mixture density networks and Capsule Networks, are employed for the inverse design and forward prediction of nanophotonic devices to optimize performance under fabrication constraints [REF:8a30f065fbb412787800aa9102e83357ecee2625][REF:76910e4dc401166d1ed020de2dea552bd4b2b26e][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Deep learning-based inverse design methods, including generative models and representation learning, are increasingly used to address under-constrained or dynamically evolving photonic device optimization problems [REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1][REF:aed4e02073734bc74e53063c0b555f881cc3221d][REF:bb82053d4229357925b28ffe647377f56d93b06e]. Inverse design methodologies for nanophotonic devices, such as scintillators and power splitters, rely on deep learning surrogates to replace computationally expensive Monte Carlo or FDTD simulations, enabling gradient-based optimization of complex geometries [REF:df8e9aae69294b110610041b9fc591c9f3d171e7][REF:c0a2dd7d6fabe134e59ca64fa09cfbb3552f90cb][REF:0482241c1bd933adb55a6674133d1688626bdab1][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Inverse design methodologies in nanophotonics allow for the optimization of device geometries to meet specific optical responses, thereby mitigating some fabrication tolerance constraints by designing robust modes less sensitive to minor geometric variations [REF:aed4e02073734bc74e53063c0b555f881cc3221d][REF:bb82053d4229357925b28ffe647377f56d93b06e][REF:ae237e70ca4fd23980ced32406ed67668edf25c8]. These physics-informed approaches allow for the optimization of device performance despite fabrication limitations and high-dimensional design spaces [REF:fcc67514a196a07bd42ee9825edbf14be0854847][REF:037e66f37189104d61c0d000de0cee202b7eec9e][REF:8a30f065fbb412787800aa9102e83357ecee2625][REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2][REF:77474cdcf483b25f73dacf20deb295cd1c2945b7][REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1].

However, deep learning-based inverse design frameworks face generalization limitations when extrapolating beyond training datasets, requiring hybrid algorithms or foundation models like MOCLIP to learn shared latent spaces for broader applicability across diverse metasurface geometries and spectra [REF:0c04bf631766f465f0b30dcd07a342ff014ed8f3][REF:e4817fc664074ddc772d9ae198871fc6cb7f8b7b][REF:60df2f3598ea7d56ae24a649121907a6769eb444]. Heterogeneous back-end-of-line (BEOL) integration of thin-film lithium niobate onto active silicon photonics platforms enables the combination of strong Pockels electro-optic modulation with CMOS-compatible dense routing, addressing the trade-off between modulation speed and fabrication maturity [REF:9b444e84d20ae6e3d62d2d4df30268b5a53baad2]. The trade-off between CMOS compatibility and density on one hand and intrinsic material properties like the Pockels effect on the other remains unresolved for general-purpose scalable photonic computing, necessitating hybrid integration strategies to balance loss, speed, and fabrication maturity. As the field advances, the optimal material platform will likely depend on the specific computational workload, with hybrid heterogeneous integration offering a pragmatic path toward combining the best attributes of multiple material systems on a single chip.
