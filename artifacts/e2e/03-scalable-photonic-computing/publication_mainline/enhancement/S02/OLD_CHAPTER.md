Coherent Mach-Zehnder interferometer (MZI) meshes serve as the fundamental building blocks for programmable photonic computing, enabling universal unitary transformations through phase-controlled interference [REF:955db7b00b8b503d46c2749d399075e11f40c61b]. These interferometric structures decompose arbitrary N×N unitary matrices into sequences of 2×2 rotation operations, where each stage comprises directional couplers and thermo-optic or electro-optic phase shifters arranged in a cascaded topology. The choice of mesh configuration—rectangular Reck or triangular Clemente arrangements—determines the minimum component count required for full expressivity, with rectangular meshes requiring exactly N(N−1)/2 tunable elements for an N-dimensional transformation. On integrated photonic chips, MZI arrays constitute a mainstream solution for implementing optical matrix-vector multiplication alongside free-space diffraction systems and micro-ring resonator architectures [REF:f7bd07322c2336c8e12a77f9c4303d2a8768892a][REF:08c02df6c726cfff2342aa7f9eef86c10f9018f1][REF:202151bc2865805cdc47f2c100dd2002d44eece5][REF:955db7b00b8b503d46c2749d399075e11f40c61b][REF:ed14198e3579a78f1802b519db669f0ec2185d51]. The silicon photonics platform has proven particularly advantageous for these implementations due to its mature fabrication infrastructure, high refractive index contrast enabling tight bending radii, and CMOS compatibility for co-integration with electronic control circuits. However, the scalability of coherent MZI-based designs faces inherent constraints: the O(N²) component scaling rapidly becomes prohibitive for large-dimensional linear algebra operations, and the cumulative insertion loss across deep mesh hierarchies degrades signal-to-noise ratios for wide-bandwidth applications.

Diffractive optical neural networks (DONNs) implement linear transformations through physical diffraction layers, offering a static passive alternative to interferometric meshes for specific machine learning tasks [REF:18736e817b036394ff6925eefbc4305014dcde77][REF:adb51917e624e16d5f8f4c4b793878218e326bf2][REF:2bf31e06af3408f0136147bf321fdeb024f50a1b][REF:08c02df6c726cfff2342aa7f9eef86c10f9018f1][REF:202151bc2865805cdc47f2c100dd2002d44eece5][REF:955db7b00b8b503d46c2749d399075e11f40c61b][REF:ed14198e3579a78f1802b519db669f0ec2185d51]. Unlike programmable MZI meshes that require continuous electrical reconfiguration, DONNs encode weight matrices directly into the spatial geometry of diffractive surfaces, eliminating the need for active tuning elements and their associated power consumption. Photonic crystal diffractive optical neural networks (PhC-DONNs) extend this paradigm by utilizing two-dimensional photonic crystals to enable multi-wavelength parallel processing for on-chip optical classification [REF:3ba89be3519e5be4bb45c0cbc483536e1db8bb63]. By establishing a wavelength-diffraction joint modulation model, PhC-DONNs overcome the inherent limitations of traditional single-wavelength diffractive networks, demonstrating enhanced throughput through spectral multiplexing. The periodic dielectric structures of photonic crystals manipulate electromagnetic wave propagation through bandgap engineering, creating well-defined transmission windows and localized defect modes that can be precisely engineered for targeted optical responses. While DONNs sacrifice the reconfigurability of MZI meshes, their passive nature provides compelling advantages for fixed-function inference workloads, particularly in edge deployment scenarios where power budgets are severely constrained.

Advanced multiplexing strategies and integrated tensor core architectures enable scalable matrix-vector multiplication within compact photonic circuit footprints. Wavelength-time interleaving enables single-shot matrix-vector multiplication using a single Mach-Zehnder interferometer data modulator by exploiting wavelength-division multiplexing and precise dispersion management [REF:4beb72c18e050a6d48cc1880f63f36f39925d1a7][REF:08c02df6c726cfff2342aa7f9eef86c10f9018f1][REF:202151bc2865805cdc47f2c100dd2002d44eece5][REF:955db7b00b8b503d46c2749d399075e11f40c61b][REF:ed14198e3579a78f1802b519db669f0ec2185d51]. This approach dramatically reduces the hardware footprint compared to spatially parallel MZI meshes by encoding multiple vector elements across distinct wavelength channels rather than dedicated waveguide paths. Integrated photonic tensor cores utilize parallel convolutional processing architectures to implement linear algebra operations, establishing the foundational circuit-level building blocks for programmable photonic computing at scale [REF:0c5bb1f1c962af9e250c63c2d759da643968a1cd][REF:08c02df6c726cfff2342aa7f9eef86c10f9018f1][REF:202151bc2865805cdc47f2c100dd2002d44eece5][REF:955db7b00b8b503d46c2749d399075e11f40c61b][REF:ed14198e3579a78f1802b519db669f0ec2185d51]. Tensor-compressed training methods further enhance efficiency by utilizing core tensor contraction to reconstruct partial weight matrices, enabling efficient implementation of linear algebra operations within the limited number of available tunable elements [REF:e97a62ecf260cf70c53b1333213e1a24bd2704a1][REF:08c02df6c726cfff2342aa7f9eef86c10f9018f1][REF:202151bc2865805cdc47f2c100dd2002d44eece5][REF:955db7b00b8b503d46c2749d399075e11f40c61b][REF:ed14198e3579a78f1802b519db669f0ec2185d51]. These architectural innovations collectively address the scaling bottleneck of conventional MZI meshes, though they introduce new complexities in wavelength routing, dispersion compensation, and temporal synchronization that must be carefully managed in system-level designs.

Inverse design methodologies leveraging deep learning and topology optimization provide computational frameworks for synthesizing complex photonic circuit geometries that deviate from standard regular mesh architectures [REF:d077ba1e200eb573e493e87abf08c66338d1819d][REF:08c02df6c726cfff2342aa7f9eef86c10f9018f1][REF:202151bc2865805cdc47f2c100dd2002d44eece5][REF:955db7b00b8b503d46c2749d399075e11f40c61b][REF:ed14198e3579a78f1802b519db669f0ec2185d51]. Nanophotonic inverse design techniques enable the implementation of non-intuitive optical circuit topologies that overcome the limitations of intuition-based manual design, allowing for optimized mesh architectures in programmable photonic computing [REF:2326997ee1d85fcb31f51c4488cdfaa40639d131]. Inverse design techniques utilizing adjoint methods or physics-informed neural networks enable the automated synthesis of nanophotonic structures, offering an alternative to hand-crafted interferometric meshes for specific linear transformation tasks [REF:717a7acde176d0cfa323ca21c76df50c954e57bc][REF:fa86aab8916cbb533d714bce5861b01dabbf18cb][REF:08c02df6c726cfff2342aa7f9eef86c10f9018f1][REF:202151bc2865805cdc47f2c100dd2002d44eece5][REF:955db7b00b8b503d46c2749d399075e11f40c61b][REF:ed14198e3579a78f1802b519db669f0ec2185d51]. Deep neural networks serve as surrogate models for the inverse design of integrated nanophotonic devices, enabling rapid prediction of optical responses from device geometry parameters without repeated electromagnetic simulations [REF:8535f8effb032edcbbe608d47dcffb0e4fc0b7d8][REF:08c02df6c726cfff2342aa7f9eef86c10f9018f1][REF:202151bc2865805cdc47f2c100dd2002d44eece5][REF:955db7b00b8b503d46c2749d399075e11f40c61b][REF:ed14198e3579a78f1802b519db669f0ec2185d51]. Machine learning-based inverse design strategies, including conditional models and adversarial autoencoders, enable the accurate determination of optimal nanophotonic structures for specific spectral or functional targets [REF:be92b5869912808f81064f1200f66524c745c075][REF:6a3fe6e532206b5383229bc162a011e443553af1][REF:08c02df6c726cfff2342aa7f9eef86c10f9018f1][REF:202151bc2865805cdc47f2c100dd2002d44eece5][REF:955db7b00b8b503d46c2749d399075e11f40c61b][REF:ed14198e3579a78f1802b519db669f0ec2185d51]. Deep learning-based inverse design methods, including generative models and representation learning, are increasingly used to optimize nanophotonic structures for specific functionalities, addressing challenges in under-constrained or dynamically evolving design problems [REF:22583693b2e475539a86ea1680d5f148d67f6467][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1][REF:aed4e02073734bc74e53063c0b555f881cc3221d]. These data-driven approaches circumvent the analytical limitations of conventional template-based design by exploring vast geometric parameter spaces that would be intractable to navigate through human intuition alone.

Discriminative deep learning models bypass repeated electromagnetic input-output simulations, serving as efficient surrogates that reduce the computational bottlenecks inherent in traditional optimization schemes for nanophotonic device design [REF:2326997ee1d85fcb31f51c4488cdfaa40639d131][REF:fa3b1eb0aa4b3c867fe5b152981255a787851789]. When surrogate models are included in the design workflow, expensive full-wave evaluations can be reserved for only the most promising structures, making staged optimization pipelines especially valuable in EM inverse design where response-to-geometry mapping is often nonunique, highly nonlinear, and constrained by fabrication rules. Inverse-designed nanophotonic neural network accelerators utilize compact, non-mesh topologies where input features are encoded onto coherent optical fields and processed through diffractive or scattering elements to perform linear algebra operations [REF:c0a2dd7d6fabe134e59ca64fa09cfbb3552f90cb][REF:08c02df6c726cfff2342aa7f9eef86c10f9018f1][REF:202151bc2865805cdc47f2c100dd2002d44eece5][REF:955db7b00b8b503d46c2749d399075e11f40c61b][REF:ed14198e3579a78f1802b519db669f0ec2185d51]. Fabrication constraints such as minimum feature size must be explicitly enforced during the inverse design of photonic devices to ensure that the resulting circuit elements are manufacturable using standard foundry processes [REF:77dd2a80bc5f7d1504a411a519cae5c5e5074099][REF:72104901d05262d178fce1a028164d85f5fba671][REF:08c02df6c726cfff2342aa7f9eef86c10f9018f1][REF:202151bc2865805cdc47f2c100dd2002d44eece5][REF:955db7b00b8b503d46c2749d399075e11f40c61b][REF:ed14198e3579a78f1802b519db669f0ec2185d51]. Hybrid optimization pipelines that combine data-driven proposal with physics-aware constraint handling and solver-based verification represent a practical path toward deployable photonic circuits that balance nominal performance with robustness and manufacturability.
