Conventional metasurfaces deliver precise control over multiple degrees of freedom—including phase, amplitude, and polarization—at subwavelength scales, yet their optical functions are fixed at the moment of fabrication [REF:d3a8c619f7797527ae0f94573705a517d9aad779][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. This immobility restricts adaptability in applications requiring real-time scene updates, making dynamic reconfigurability one of the most actively pursued frontiers in nanophotonics. Metasurfaces have already demonstrated unprecedented ability to generate high-quality holograms and structural colors, establishing them as foundational platforms for next-generation display technologies [REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478][REF:534d7537d2b14d22159403688f4c5f0db3ea42ac][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Within this landscape, metalenses—ultrathin, integrable arrays of subwavelength nanostructures—provide a particularly promising architecture for dynamic holographic modulation, as they manipulate light fields through carefully engineered geometric and propagation phases without the bulk of conventional optics [REF:54e53e2aba40ccb8b0f1fa5b22deb4dfe9614917][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Transitioning from static to active holographic operation therefore requires coupling these nanostructured elements with external stimuli that can alter local electromagnetic response in situ.

Several material platforms have emerged as candidates for embedding active modulation into metasurface holographic devices. Phase-change materials (PCMs) such as germanium–antimony–telluride (GST) and vanadium dioxide (VO2) offer non-volatile refractive-index switching triggered by electrical current, optical pulses, or thermal cycling, providing a pathway to rewrite holographic patterns with microsecond-to-millisecond response times. Dynamic nonlocal metasurfaces incorporating PCMs enable multifunctional integration by modifying the structural parameters of individual metaatoms—for example, adjusting rotation angles—to achieve continuously tunable optical responses across distinct holographic states [REF:4858131ccdb1b5a8b654f10980675a9bccf0db9b][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. The non-volatility of PCM switching offers an energy advantage over purely resistive or capacitive tuning schemes, as the holographic state persists without continuous power input. Nevertheless, the finite crystallization and amorphization kinetics of chalcogenide glasses impose intrinsic speed limits, and repeated phase transitions can induce material fatigue that degrades diffraction efficiency over extended cycling.

The computational challenge of designing dynamically tunable metasurface holograms has catalyzed the adoption of inverse design frameworks that map desired far-field interference patterns directly to feasible meta-atom geometries. Traditional pattern-to-phase paradigms suffer from severe computational bottlenecks when addressing the non-uniqueness inherent in mapping target holographic fields back to physical structures [REF:c9e5de816ca2356737e7f23532658ef28e6b2738]. Heuristic optimization methods often produce target phase profiles or transfer matrices that are not physically realizable; when idealized designs are projected onto manufacturable metasurface architectures, the resulting mismatch causes significant deviations in reconstructed image quality. Inverse design frameworks, including adjoint-based gradient evaluation and deep learning models, enable the generation of complex metasurface geometries that are subsequently binarized for fabrication feasibility [REF:a801ee6d75d827ef0b99528619e877e3aac72277][REF:e4817fc664074ddc772d9ae198871fc6cb7f8b7b][REF:60df2f3598ea7d56ae24a649121907a6769eb444]. These frameworks also permit the incorporation of fabrication constraints—such as minimum feature sizes—directly into the training objective, ensuring that generated geometries remain compatible with lithographic processes.

Deep learning surrogate models and differentiable simulators have dramatically reduced the computational cost of optimizing metasurface geometries for dynamic holographic applications. Early inverse design approaches relied on fully connected neural networks to approximate forward scattering functions, replacing slow electromagnetic solvers but failing to solve the inverse problem directly [REF:9c20a790aa75f24b6f5a130711645c978484455e][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Subsequent architectures, including tandem neural networks and generative adversarial networks, improved upon this by minimizing prediction error of re-simulated spectra rather than geometric distance alone. More recently, spatial coupled mode theory combined with differentiable neural simulators has enabled computationally efficient optimization of metasurface geometries for spectral imaging and holographic applications, achieving order-of-magnitude reductions in design cycle time compared to full-wave electromagnetic simulations [REF:4cad266be07154f9e36a00e0ca18bf6c548b483f][REF:76a83ea3baed75ad88b7f0782eb9a694a330c853][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303].

Beyond single-function tuning, machine-learning-guided designs enable multifunctional metasurfaces that simultaneously modulate multiple degrees of freedom. Elastically reconfigurable metasurfaces designed via data-driven machine learning can modulate mass or stiffness distributions to cover a full 2π phase shift span while maintaining impedance matching across operating bands [REF:e5fdf745904a5e9a10a69f62589f2245dc810ed8][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Machine learning and optimization approaches further allow independent control of non-uniform bianisotropic metasurface unit cells, enabling complex electromagnetic responses such as polarization-dependent three-dimensional holography through inverse design nanoprinting [REF:1dccfff88ca81c606d5447a3ae21626e072e01be][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) metasurfaces can similarly be inverse-designed using deep convolutional networks to control resonance intensities and scattering field interference through precisely tuned current flow in resonator ring structures [REF:09cb7b0a5114f90d8546fdc7032e1473aa24bb8e][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Together, these capabilities expand the functional space available to dynamic holographic systems beyond simple intensity or phase switching toward simultaneous control of polarization state, focal length, and holographic content.

Despite these advances, fundamental trade-offs between modulation speed, diffraction efficiency, and spectral bandwidth persist across all active modulation platforms. Inverse design frameworks for metasurfaces, including physics-informed diffusion models and deep learning approaches, address the ill-posed nature of determining physical structures from desired spectral responses, though their application to rigorous spectral constraints in dynamic holography remains an active area of investigation [REF:9c20a790aa75f24b6f5a130711645c978484455e][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Closing the loop between computational design fidelity, material switching speed, and optical throughput represents the central engineering challenge for bringing dynamic metasurface holography from laboratory demonstrations to practical display and sensing applications.
