Metasurface holographic displays are primarily implemented using transmissive or reflective architectures that serve as the core wavefront modulation element within complete display systems [REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. These configurations leverage subwavelength nanostructures to achieve precise control over multiple degrees of freedom—including phase, amplitude, and polarization—at scales far below the operating wavelength. By locally designing individual nanoantennas across the metasurface plane, holographic wavefront shaping becomes possible within micro- and nanoscale thicknesses, overcoming the inherent limitations of traditional refractive and diffractive optical components in terms of size, integration density, and functional complexity. Recent demonstrations have integrated these metasurfaces into full display systems capable of both static and dynamic holographic projection, marking a transition from isolated component studies toward system-level evaluation.

Hybrid meta-refractive lens architectures, which combine metasurfaces for aberration correction with conventional refractive elements providing optical power, represent a viable integration pathway for compact holographic display systems [REF:d54b94b2ee8549d7ab4bf6ec47e0259df06a047a][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. This hybrid approach decouples the focusing function from the wavefront correction function, enabling more flexible system designs. Complementing this strategy, achromatic metasurface waveguides employing ultra-thin silicon nitride nanostructure couplers offer a relatively simple and cost-effective fabrication route for full-color augmented reality displays [REF:94ab4c73d8ea40f73635551d123a18a300d824cb][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Such waveguide-based implementations aim to project holographic imagery directly into the user's eye while maintaining broad spectral response, addressing one of the persistent challenges in AR display color fidelity.

The design of complex metasurface holograms relies fundamentally on computational inverse design frameworks that address the ill-posed nature of the inverse problem—mapping desired spectral and phase responses back to physical nanostructure geometries [REF:9c20a790aa75f24b6f5a130711645c978484455e][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Traditional intuition-based design approaches cannot navigate the high-dimensional parameter spaces required for sophisticated holographic wavefront control. Instead, inverse design methods including deep learning and heuristic optimization algorithms have been deployed to create the complex wavefront modulation elements essential for holographic displays [REF:2d3412a215f8b6d1d395bfaeec7ec0379c2e132e][REF:2326997ee1d85fcb31f51c4488cdfaa40639d131]. Computational frameworks utilizing physics-informed diffusion models and machine learning optimization enable efficient exploration by approximating forward scattering functions and optimizing cost functions for desired output fields [REF:2cae66f6687383743873a573e20b2d6b327f8538][REF:2a3fb330497fc0883c9a7acc35de2b94ad7c1af0][REF:9c20a790aa75f24b6f5a130711645c978484455e][REF:identity-fallback:e49491a3a2a5b595][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Methodologies incorporating spatial coupled mode theory and adjoint-driven optimization further enable systematic generation of complex metasurface holograms by solving high-dimensional nonconvex design problems that prove intractable for conventional heuristic methods [REF:4cad266be07154f9e36a00e0ca18bf6c548b483f][REF:76a83ea3baed75ad88b7f0782eb9a694a330c853][REF:1dccfff88ca81c606d5447a3ae21626e072e01be][REF:c176542f7131ecf16a4fbb3f7423615a407f6266][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303].

Deep learning techniques and hybrid optimization algorithms have become essential tools for navigating the high-dimensional design spaces inherent in metasurface holographic systems. Physics-informed machine learning integrates governing physical equations directly into neural network architectures—through loss function incorporation, residual modeling, and architectural initialization—to improve both prediction accuracy and the implementability of inverse-designed optical metamaterials [REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1][REF:0d03f30ab91ec0b9411c3e2505550e636df8808e]. Because neural networks struggle to generalize beyond their training datasets, hybrid algorithms combining data-driven learning with heuristic or rigorous simulation methods have emerged to overcome these generalization limitations [REF:c9e5de816ca2356737e7f23532658ef28e6b2738][REF:0c04bf631766f465f0b30dcd07a342ff014ed8f3][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Generative models and deep learning surrogate models play a critical role in the inverse design of metasurfaces under multi-physics coupling, enabling out-of-range predictions and efficient exploration of the design space for complex display functionalities [REF:bde09ee292448b5314e6a37f9a4a22ef32832ec5][REF:fa86aab8916cbb533d714bce5861b01dabbf18cb][REF:0196bb9874e9d2ccf041c735b0dc90b7d1183390][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Deep learning-based inverse design enables the realization of complex metasurface functionalities such as polarization control, electromagnetically induced transparency, and sub-diffraction focusing by mapping desired electromagnetic responses directly to structural parameters [REF:09cb7b0a5114f90d8546fdc7032e1473aa24bb8e][REF:a52cc1154f412a7274d108ad735e0bec014d002f][REF:e29144a60ad5b45d897fa18bce4f835c765d822f][REF:b42bd38e555180785a3caccc5ad50695d198d4c7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. However, the one-to-many mapping relationship between input electromagnetic responses and output structural parameters necessitates robust dataset curation and training strategies to ensure unique and manufacturable solutions [REF:a52cc1154f412a7274d108ad735e0bec014d002f][REF:9fd9affc4636c62b62cb7aff31d070f1337f6ce8][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Deep learning and inverse design methods including transfer learning and multi-task neural networks are being adopted to automate the complex numerical optimization of metasurface holograms, reducing reliance on manual pattern-to-phase design paradigms [REF:696cef328f3171c7583907984fa71c43adc0e828][REF:9d2f38e81909f3b55bd54ccc38fdc11afffa416c][REF:3cddd1d9bd3d6090a98fccc324fd43ea0278042f][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Inverse design of metasurfaces for display applications relies on high-dimensional nonlinear optimization frameworks, including gradient descent initialized with heuristic designs and neural network-based approaches, to achieve specified electromagnetic responses [REF:59ff8f74aae2a26a0eff13f9d82dc3c080121717][REF:232942d5ddb877df3c6df804b150911462d17431][REF:9bfb2ed7a07dd7f23eeec49c9ae1cf507ced5446][REF:caf12afcfca29d36b1269091c911c4ae931bb88e][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Iterative electromagnetic metamaterial inverse design methods are categorized into gradient-based and gradient-free branches, with discriminative models bypassing repeated electromagnetic simulations to improve efficiency [REF:2d3412a215f8b6d1d395bfaeec7ec0379c2e132e][REF:2326997ee1d85fcb31f51c4488cdfaa40639d131][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Furthermore, inverse design of metasurfaces for display applications is often under-constrained or evolves dynamically due to shifting objectives and fabrication limits, requiring representation learning to mitigate these challenges [REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Latent optimization and machine learning methods compress high-dimensional design spaces to address limitations in traditional inverse design paradigms for metasurface components [REF:534d7537d2b14d22159403688f4c5f0db3ea42ac][REF:2cae66f6687383743873a573e20b2d6b327f8538][REF:1af94fcc4f5e3ebcd28f6d5aaecaedcebb0d612b][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303].

Advanced inverse design enables multi-channel metasurface holographic architectures that generate multiple independent holographic outputs through polarization, wavelength, or frequency multiplexing. Deep-learning-assisted inverse design has enabled the creation of dual-spin/frequency metasurfaces capable of generating quad-channel off-axis holographic outputs, demonstrating a clear pathway to complex multi-wavelength or multi-polarization display architectures [REF:867c87ae195e0a5c2016732e0f43a8c703a7a788][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Inverse design frameworks utilizing machine learning—including combined loss functions fitting Fourier representations and asymmetric CycleGANs—enable the automated generation of complex metasurface geometries required for high-fidelity holographic wavefront control [REF:ab2c0490cf291b46e0ddc2053744f03dd52a5223][REF:fd569438fd58ed1920ae43be4366bccc68ae96b0][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Additionally, inverse design methodologies including adjoint optimization and machine learning enable the correction of chromatic and geometric aberrations in metasurface-based imaging systems by co-optimizing the wavefront and the metasurface structure [REF:eb05cd6cdf1599f09588017b728231838003c121][REF:9667eeb1c5b558ad616938eebac8ff3586ce96c0][REF:2066503cfc076298771d675eae0ad855e62aec67][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303].

Increasing metasurface design complexity introduces crosstalk between channels and reduces work efficiency, representing a key barrier to high-performance display integration [REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Compounding this challenge, the inverse design of nanophotonic structures faces non-uniqueness problems where different meta-atom geometries yield equivalent optical properties, complicating deterministic manufacturing for display applications [REF:e4817fc664074ddc772d9ae198871fc6cb7f8b7b][REF:60df2f3598ea7d56ae24a649121907a6769eb444][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. These fundamental constraints mean that even when optimal designs are computationally identified, translating them into reliable, scalable fabrication processes remains an open engineering challenge.

Dynamic metasurface display demonstrations indicate varying levels of application readiness across different domains. Hybrid metal-dielectric metasurfaces exhibiting bound states in the continuum support high-quality factor resonances and electric field confinement that can be dynamically modulated via symmetry breaking in geometry, offering potential pathways for active display operation [REF:cc73f5040c5d928b3ca5aeeb38e0df04450efc53][REF:42f2fcc893f3a45ac750a8ffe67752f8533c0c61][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Neural nano-optics and inverse design algorithms enable the computational generation of complex metasurface phase profiles for high-quality holographic imaging, shifting the design bottleneck from analytical derivation to data-driven optimization [REF:717a7acde176d0cfa323ca21c76df50c954e57bc][REF:81807791bd6fda88a9b1d39fef86a92f599a2ec4][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Microfluidics-enabled metasurfaces have been demonstrated as dynamically adaptive camouflage cloaks capable of broadband, multi-illusion operation, representing a specific application niche for dynamic metasurface displays [REF:2a1ac67eb9a6793168e50de6831ae02c5372b6b6][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. The feasibility of inverse-designed metalenses for sub-diffraction focusing is validated by consistency between topological optimization results, particle swarm optimization outcomes, and theoretical values, confirming the practical viability of computational design pipelines [REF:e29144a60ad5b45d897fa18bce4f835c765d822f][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Deep learning and active learning frameworks are increasingly employed to navigate the vast design spaces of nanofabricated metasurfaces, facilitating identification of optimal structures for desired optical responses in display components [REF:c150b149a6938f08d8cc56b4815ae51a7d8b0aa0][REF:236a6053b2a6e3a2cf09f9c29235e7c0fe78cb3c][REF:684e6146d2b5c24b21821f45d46f27bde809c776][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Adjoint optimization techniques allow for the inverse design of randomly reduced metalenses, offering a pathway to simplify fabrication constraints while maintaining performance in integrated optical systems [REF:49c2e2a15f9a438dd7c1b0ef12d7e09884e0e88f][REF:77195692abbb5942ffe39c2406cbb8586e0575d8][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Physics-informed neural networks and reinforcement learning are employed to optimize metasurface device designs, addressing the computational complexity of inverse design for complex multifunctional optical elements [REF:77dd2a80bc5f7d1504a411a519cae5c5e5074099][REF:bde09ee292448b5314e6a37f9a4a22ef32832ec5][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202110463][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303]. Despite these advances, quantitative performance benchmarks comparing metasurface holographic displays against competing technologies such as spatial light modulator-based and volumetric approaches remain notably sparse in the literature, leaving several open questions regarding real-world applicability thresholds.
