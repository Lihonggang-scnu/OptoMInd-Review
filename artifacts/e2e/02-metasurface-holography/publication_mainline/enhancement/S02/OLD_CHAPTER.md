Inverse design methods for nanophotonics have evolved from intuition-based heuristics to mathematical optimization and deep learning, enabling the exploration of high-dimensional design spaces to achieve complex optical functionalities that are difficult to realize with analytical approaches [REF:2d3412a215f8b6d1d395bfaeec7ec0379c2e132e][REF:2326997ee1d85fcb31f51c4488cdfaa40639d131]. Computational inverse design for metasurface holograms has undergone this transformation, evolving from analytical superposition methods to algorithmic optimization techniques including adjoint gradient methods, topology optimization, and deep learning-based approaches [REF:2cae66f6687383743873a573e20b2d6b327f8538][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. These techniques overcome the limitation of intuition-based designs, boosting the implementation of non-intuitive optical devices ranging from beam splitters to metalenses, optical cloaks, reflective metasurfaces, and polarization-sensitive displays. The first discussed optimization methods represent the oldest and perhaps most intuitive applications of inverse design, where binary representations and re-parameterization of design structures in topology optimization iterate successive figure-of-merit minimization until reaching an optimal solution.

Phase modulation designs often rely on precomputed phase libraries or lookup tables that map required phase shifts to specific nanostructure geometries, serving as a foundational but limited encoding strategy [REF:c176542f7131ecf16a4fbb3f7423615a407f6266][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Inverse design for metasurface holograms is formulated as an optimization problem in a high-dimensional, nonconvex space, where traditional heuristic methods like particle swarm optimization and genetic algorithms are employed to navigate the search landscape [REF:76a83ea3baed75ad88b7f0782eb9a694a330c853][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. However, heuristic optimization methods often generate target phase profiles or transfer matrices that are not physically realizable, creating a mismatch when idealized designs are projected onto feasible metasurface structures [REF:c9e5de816ca2356737e7f23532658ef28e6b2738]. This disconnect between abstract models and physical reality has been a persistent challenge; efforts to mitigate this issue, such as applying smoothness regularization to phase profiles to ease hardware design, have generally been inadequate since smoother profiles limit expressivity without ensuring true physical feasibility.

Gradient-based inverse design algorithms, including topology optimization and adjoint methods, optimize nanostructure geometries by computing gradients of an objective function with respect to structural parameters, enabling the mapping of desired electromagnetic performance to physical layouts [REF:e29144a60ad5b45d897fa18bce4f835c765d822f][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Adjoint-driven inverse design methods enable the optimization of complex multi-spectral metasurfaces by efficiently computing gradients with respect to design variables, allowing for precise wavefront shaping across multiple wavelengths [REF:c176542f7131ecf16a4fbb3f7423615a407f6266][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Gradient-based topology optimization leverages the design parameter space of material distribution within a simulation domain to optimize nanophotonic structures, allowing for arbitrary dispersive materials in broadband three-dimensional contexts [REF:6898fb9b38a16056d0997abe1c40d3dcfc2c0b82][REF:1af94fcc4f5e3ebcd28f6d5aaecaedcebb0d612b][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Continuous profiles generated by freeform optimization must be properly binarized during the process to reach feasible results for fabrication, introducing a discretization step that may affect the final optical performance [REF:a801ee6d75d827ef0b99528619e877e3aac72277].

The advent of deep learning introduced a paradigm shift in inverse design by utilizing fully connected neural networks to approximate forward scattering functions, thereby replacing slow electromagnetic solvers [REF:9c20a790aa75f24b6f5a130711645c978484455e][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Deep neural networks, including tandem and residual architectures, have been applied to inverse design of metasurfaces to replace time-consuming numerical calculations with rapid forward prediction and pattern generation [REF:684e6146d2b5c24b21821f45d46f27bde809c776][REF:696cef328f3171c7583907984fa71c43adc0e828][REF:534bc16c467c94bae9323a7fa64364a7c5ee3676][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Deep learning inverse design models, such as those using tandem architectures or foundation models like MOCLIP, learn nonlinear mappings between target optical responses and metasurface geometries by training on large datasets of simulated electromagnetic interactions [REF:e4817fc664074ddc772d9ae198871fc6cb7f8b7b][REF:60df2f3598ea7d56ae24a649121907a6769eb444][REF:a881bff2781bda627de54299c45a6708d145c27a][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Neural network-based surrogate models enable rapid inverse design of metasurfaces by replacing computationally expensive electromagnetic simulations with learned mappings from target optical responses to nanostructure geometries [REF:f6c5ab3065e6af6cf5bb768ac125248fe61fc39d][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Machine learning approaches are utilized in metasurface inverse design to address the high computational resource and time requirements of conventional methods, enabling efficient on-demand design [REF:769fda505ee33beb18834be9732beba738e47bb5][REF:9bd46bce86e7a33912554a5a16dc8385371ee4d2][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Once trained, artificial intelligence models can generate optimized designs quickly, making them suitable for real-time or large-scale inverse design tasks compared to iterative numerical solvers [REF:aed4e02073734bc74e53063c0b555f881cc3221d].

Generative models, particularly diffusion models and reinforcement learning frameworks, are increasingly applied to inverse design to improve sample efficiency and reduce the number of iterations required for convergence [REF:fa86aab8916cbb533d714bce5861b01dabbf18cb][REF:77dd2a80bc5f7d1504a411a519cae5c5e5074099][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Inverse design for metasurfaces is fundamentally an ill-posed problem requiring regularization or constraints, such as spectral filters in physics-informed diffusion models, to map desired responses to physical structures [REF:9c20a790aa75f24b6f5a130711645c978484455e][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Neural networks cannot generalize well beyond the information available in their training dataset, necessitating hybrid algorithms that combine data-driven approaches with other techniques to improve robustness and generalizability [REF:0c04bf631766f465f0b30dcd07a342ff014ed8f3]. Transfer learning enables inverse design networks to generalize across heterogeneous metasurface datasets with distinct functionalities by fine-tuning specific network parameters while freezing others, reducing the need for extensive retraining on new design tasks [REF:3cddd1d9bd3d6090a98fccc324fd43ea0278042f][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478].

Inverse design methods exploit high degrees of freedom including complex 2D subwavelength geometries and multilayer thin film stacking to control light-matter interaction beyond simple phase modulation [REF:42e53c9c7630eb735f9a67f329c948423910cd8d][REF:534d7537d2b14d22159403688f4c5f0db3ea42ac][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Inverse design of metasurfaces aims to directly determine optimal nanostructure geometries that achieve desired electromagnetic responses, addressing limitations of incomplete design specifications and fabrication constraints inherent in traditional forward-design approaches [REF:39f14359d82511b9283dbcdaaeb9cbdbb08e2db2][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Fast direct solvers enable full-wave inverse design of cm-scale cylindrical metasurfaces, allowing computational optimization of large-area holographic elements that were previously intractable due to simulation cost [REF:717a7acde176d0cfa323ca21c76df50c954e57bc][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. Data-driven frameworks utilizing differentiable neural simulators map metasurface geometries directly to spectral responses, enabling inverse design for hyperspectral imaging applications [REF:76a83ea3baed75ad88b7f0782eb9a694a330c853][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478].

Inverse design algorithms typically operate on idealized nanostructure models that assume perfect geometric precision, creating a potential design-reality gap where fabrication-induced disorder or material variations may degrade the predicted holographic performance [REF:doi:10.1038/s41377-023-01260-w][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. The reliance on specific resonance modes such as symmetry-protected bound states in the continuum (BICs) and quasi-BICs in inverse design imposes strict constraints on the degrees of freedom available to the algorithm, limiting the flexibility of the resulting device designs [REF:4a7069f2a07e502040760df66b8687901e360257][REF:4858131ccdb1b5a8b654f10980675a9bccf0db9b][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478]. The evolution of inverse design methods reflects a shift from simple analytical encoding to sophisticated algorithmic optimization, yet these methods typically operate on idealized models that may not fully capture fabrication-induced disorder [REF:76a83ea3baed75ad88b7f0782eb9a694a330c853][REF:1dccfff88ca81c606d5447a3ae21626e072e01be][REF:c176542f7131ecf16a4fbb3f7423615a407f6266][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:04b54013e0f88ceff799e9ea43e209862b7aa998][REF:14ea4dd97f8227fbe71b806c4574c75050a418dc][REF:dfa21cd70a71ab37879ca600843b21680f16e478].
