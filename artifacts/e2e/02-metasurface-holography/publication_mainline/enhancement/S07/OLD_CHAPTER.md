The field of metasurface holography has reached an inflection point where the fundamental challenge is no longer isolated performance optimization but the resolution of deeply coupled mechanism trade-offs that span design, fabrication, and system integration. Inverse design frameworks for metasurface holography face a fundamental tension between achieving high-fidelity phase control and managing fabrication disorder, where joint optimization of the inverse problem with fabrication tolerance constraints is emerging as a necessary co-design strategy to resolve this coupled mechanism bottleneck [REF:ab2c0490cf291b46e0ddc2053744f03dd52a5223][REF:39f14359d82511b9283dbcdaaeb9cbdbb08e2db2][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:doi:10.1038/s41377-025-02014-6][REF:doi:10.3390/nano14010066][REF:doi:10.1117/1.ap.7.5.056004]. The inverse design of metasurfaces for holography is fundamentally an ill-posed problem due to the non-uniqueness of structure-to-response mappings, necessitating sparsity assumptions or physics-informed constraints to enable computationally tractable solutions [REF:9c20a790aa75f24b6f5a130711645c978484455e][REF:identity-fallback:e49491a3a2a5b595][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:doi:10.1038/s41377-025-02014-6][REF:doi:10.3390/nano14010066][REF:doi:10.1117/1.ap.7.5.056004]. These observations collectively signal that the field's progress depends less on incremental improvements to individual design components than on resolving the interdependencies that couple electromagnetic response, manufacturability, and application-specific performance requirements.

At the heart of this challenge lies the non-uniqueness of structure-to-response mappings in inverse design, which renders metasurface hologram synthesis an inherently ill-posed optimization problem in high-dimensional parameter spaces. Inverse design of metasurfaces exhibits a non-uniqueness problem where distinct meta-atom geometries can yield equivalent optical properties, complicating the mapping between desired phase profiles and physical structures [REF:60df2f3598ea7d56ae24a649121907a6769eb444]. This multiplicity of valid solutions means that the inverse design of metasurfaces for tailored optical functions is an inherently nonconvex optimization problem in high-dimensional parameter spaces, where traditional heuristic methods like genetic algorithms and particle swarm optimization often struggle to find global optima compared to modern gradient-based or machine-learning approaches [REF:76a83ea3baed75ad88b7f0782eb9a694a330c853][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:doi:10.1038/s41377-025-02014-6][REF:doi:10.3390/nano14010066][REF:doi:10.1117/1.ap.7.5.056004]. The resulting landscape of competing solutions demands new methodological frameworks capable of navigating both the mathematical complexity and the physical realizability constraints simultaneously.

Deep neural networks and machine learning frameworks have emerged as primary mechanisms to overcome the computational bottlenecks of traditional numerical inverse design in metasurface holography, enabling rapid forward prediction and pattern-to-phase mapping that would otherwise require prohibitive full-wave electromagnetic simulations [REF:696cef328f3171c7583907984fa71c43adc0e828][REF:9d2f38e81909f3b55bd54ccc38fdc11afffa416c][REF:769fda505ee33beb18834be9732beba738e47bb5][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:doi:10.1038/s41377-025-02014-6][REF:doi:10.3390/nano14010066][REF:doi:10.1117/1.ap.7.5.056004]. This bifurcation reflects a broader methodological tension: while gradient-based methods offer fast convergence within well-characterized regions of design space, they risk premature convergence to local optima in the highly non-convex landscapes typical of realistic metasurface problems.

A critical insight emerging from recent work is that purely data-driven black-box optimization must be augmented with physics-informed constraints to ensure physical consistency and mitigate fabrication sensitivity. Integrating domain knowledge, such as governing physical equations including Maxwell's equations, into machine learning models through loss functions, residual modeling, or parameter initialization improves the physical consistency and generalizability of inverse-designed metasurfaces [REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1][REF:0d03f30ab91ec0b9411c3e2505550e636df8808e]. Inverse design frameworks for metasurface holography are increasingly integrating physics-informed constraints, such as centrosymmetric index profiles or coupled-mode theory approximations, to reduce the computational search space and mitigate fabrication sensitivity compared to purely data-driven black-box optimization [REF:8ab7d5638952e89b5ac01f349668839fd258d693][REF:717a7acde176d0cfa323ca21c76df50c954e57bc][REF:4cad266be07154f9e36a00e0ca18bf6c548b483f][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:doi:10.1038/s41377-025-02014-6][REF:doi:10.3390/nano14010066][REF:doi:10.1117/1.ap.7.5.056004]. These findings underscore that the most effective inverse design strategies combine the expressive power of data-driven models with hard constraints derived from electromagnetic theory and manufacturing capabilities.

The path forward requires a unified co-design agenda that treats the coupled mechanism bottleneck not as a collection of independent subproblems but as an integrated pipeline spanning computation, fabrication tolerance, and system-level optical performance. Resolving this bottleneck will demand methodological frameworks that natively support multi-objective optimization across electromagnetic, mechanical, and computational domains, alongside standardized benchmarks for evaluating design-to-fabrication fidelity. Only through such integrated approaches can metasurface holography transition from laboratory demonstrations to robust, scalable technologies.
