Metasurface-integrated imaging systems and optical computing architectures extend metasurface holography well beyond display applications into computational imaging, miniaturized microscopy, beam steering, and photonic computation. Inverse design of metasurfaces for computational imaging and optical computing applications is increasingly enabled by deep learning frameworks that bypass iterative electromagnetic simulations, allowing rapid generation of complex nanostructures with specified holographic or wavefront-shaping responses [REF:7bcf53e9efa7d06f99f02cb0b9f735358ce1f437][REF:232942d5ddb877df3c6df804b150911462d17431][REF:016a3c126b25de88ca5a838e2c8a0f1fc9ff5e40][REF:59ff8f74aae2a26a0eff13f9d82dc3c080121717][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Inverse design algorithms, including gradient-based topology optimization and deep learning frameworks, are employed to engineer metasurface holograms for computational imaging and optical computing applications where precise wavefront control is required [REF:2516273dc5b4511c838bf9236a61f71f4e32f6b5][REF:9fd9affc4636c62b62cb7aff31d070f1337f6ce8][REF:e29144a60ad5b45d897fa18bce4f835c765d822f][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. This transition from traditional pattern-to-phase paradigms to physics-informed computational design has opened new frontiers in which metasurface elements serve as integral components of computational imaging pipelines rather than standalone optical components.

Inverse design frameworks enable the creation of high-complexity metasurface holograms for computational imaging by navigating high-dimensional design spaces that intuition-based approaches cannot reach [REF:2d3412a215f8b6d1d395bfaeec7ec0379c2e132e][REF:2326997ee1d85fcb31f51c4488cdfaa40639d131][REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1]. Computational inverse design frameworks enable the optimization of cascaded metasurface systems by evaluating output intensity and cost functions, allowing for the compensation of system-level aberrations in imaging applications [REF:2cae66f6687383743873a573e20b2d6b327f8538][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. The integration of metasurface holography into computational imaging systems requires reconstruction algorithms that compensate for inherent metasurface aberrations, leveraging the high degrees of freedom in metasurface design to encode complex wavefronts [REF:ddb0c5f145c2112ff2f184947d55597e30368231]. Together, these capabilities allow computational imaging systems to extract information content that would be inaccessible through conventional passive optics alone.

However, hybrid algorithms combining neural networks with rigorous physical simulations are necessary to ensure that inverse-designed metasurfaces yield implementable responses and avoid performance degradation caused by oversimplified transfer matrix models [REF:c9e5de816ca2356737e7f23532658ef28e6b2738][REF:0c04bf631766f465f0b30dcd07a342ff014ed8f3][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Data-driven machine learning methods, including deep learning and diffusion models, have streamlined the inverse design process for complex metasurfaces by approximating forward scattering functions or optimizing latent spaces, replacing slower electromagnetic solvers [REF:2a3fb330497fc0883c9a7acc35de2b94ad7c1af0][REF:9c20a790aa75f24b6f5a130711645c978484455e][REF:identity-fallback:e49491a3a2a5b595][REF:0196bb9874e9d2ccf041c735b0dc90b7d1183390][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Deep learning-based inverse design methods, including neural network surrogates and active learning, enable the efficient optimization of metasurface geometries for complex wavefront manipulation tasks such as holography and metalens fabrication [REF:f6c5ab3065e6af6cf5bb768ac125248fe61fc39d][REF:c150b149a6938f08d8cc56b4815ae51a7d8b0aa0][REF:236a6053b2a6e3a2cf09f9c29235e7c0fe78cb3c][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. These methodological advances address the non-convex optimization landscape inherent in metasurface design while maintaining physical realizability constraints.

End-to-end metasurface design frameworks co-optimize optical hardware with nonlinear regression algorithms, such as Planck-regression, to perform specific computational imaging tasks like temperature map sensing from thermal radiation [REF:a17024e845e2f6fd28263e4cb1b08419b14c0cc3][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Neural nano-optics enables high-quality thin lens imaging by using inverse design to optimize metasurface structures for specific imaging tasks, compensating for aberrations through computational co-design [REF:717a7acde176d0cfa323ca21c76df50c954e57bc][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Inverse design of metasurfaces for computational imaging and optical computing is often formulated as an ill-posed problem requiring physics-informed constraints or deep learning models to map desired spectral or phase responses to physical nanostructure geometries [REF:9c20a790aa75f24b6f5a130711645c978484455e][REF:identity-fallback:e49491a3a2a5b595][REF:717a7acde176d0cfa323ca21c76df50c954e57bc][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Such end-to-end co-design approaches demonstrate that metasurfaces on their own may not produce usable images but become powerful when integrated with appropriate computational reconstruction.

Metalens-based imaging systems utilize computational reconstruction algorithms to compensate for inherent metasurface aberrations, enabling high-resolution miniaturized microscopy and holographic imaging beyond the limits of passive optical elements [REF:8b64c99ad5b39c0a6cd716d0ec4af0a1d4fb2367][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Metalenses offer opportunities for compact, stable, high-resolution three-dimensional tomography without moving parts, expanding beyond traditional display applications into advanced imaging modalities [REF:7ff7fc3fb98bf281dac7ea9ae79ad55b301bb23e][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Monolithic integration of metalenses enables minimalist optical systems for achromatic imaging within an extended field of view, addressing the mechanism-coupling challenge of chromatic aberration through physical design rather than computational post-processing [REF:54e53e2aba40ccb8b0f1fa5b22deb4dfe9614917][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. These developments demonstrate how metasurface holography can enable imaging platforms that were previously impractical due to bulk, weight, or alignment complexity.

Advanced metasurface imaging modalities further illustrate the breadth of application-specific designs. Chip-integrated metasurface sensors enable full-Stokes polarimetric imaging by simultaneously measuring all polarization states on a single chip, facilitating compact computational imaging systems [REF:doi:10.1038/s41377-023-01260-w][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. High-numerical-aperture aplanatic metalenses leverage large diffractive chromatic dispersion for spectral imaging, enabling wavelength-dependent focus tuning and compact hyperspectral imaging capabilities within a single planar element [REF:8b64c99ad5b39c0a6cd716d0ec4af0a1d4fb2367][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Multi-element metasurface systems composed of triplets can characterize 3D optical intensity distributions, demonstrating the capability of cascaded metasurfaces for complex wavefront shaping required in advanced computational imaging setups [REF:a148605d4ba86a5a0712cb63c088900b39561267][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Nonlocal Huygens' meta-lenses enable high-quality-factor spin-multiplexed imaging by decoupling the phase and amplitude responses of individual meta-atoms, allowing independent control over orthogonal circular polarizations for multi-channel holographic reconstruction [REF:2a537b9b3aec2380ca8343d8bd5cc70e6d974970][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Global angle-multiplexed metasurfaces utilize diffractive neural networks to achieve holographic imaging with multiple viewing angles, expanding the field of view in compact imaging systems without mechanical scanning components [REF:02c52d6f45a53580144850d4df5677f3c9e63ae6][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6].

Metasurface holographic beam steerers deployed in LiDAR and free-space optical communication exploit multiplexed holographic designs to achieve rapid scanning without mechanical components. Deep-learning-assisted inverse design enables the creation of dual-spin/frequency metasurfaces capable of quad-channel off-axis holography, allowing independent control over multiple degrees of freedom for complex computational imaging tasks [REF:867c87ae195e0a5c2016732e0f43a8c703a7a788][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Metasurfaces provide off-axis optical aberration correction for reflectance imaging in confocal scanning fibre endoscopy, improving image quality in miniaturized medical imaging probes [REF:83b6d6523c4d30208870b774f116709b04fd7b4b][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Hybrid meta-optics that combine metasurfaces with refractive elements can correct optical aberrations while maintaining large spatial-spectral bandwidths, enabling applications in thermal imaging and augmented reality [REF:da22d2c0d7f07d72d16ef8e639439f0d0b8387dd][REF:d54b94b2ee8549d7ab4bf6ec47e0259df06a047a][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. These beam steering and sensing applications share common design challenges with imaging systems, particularly around chromatic management and multi-degree-of-freedom control.

Optical computing architectures leverage metasurface holographic elements for matrix multiplication, Fourier processing, and neural network inference through physics-informed inverse design. Deep learning inverse design paradigms, including foundation models and transfer learning architectures, facilitate the rapid realization of functional metasurfaces for complex imaging and computing applications by solving non-uniqueness in inverse scattering problems [REF:e4817fc664074ddc772d9ae198871fc6cb7f8b7b][REF:60df2f3598ea7d56ae24a649121907a6769eb444][REF:a881bff2781bda627de54299c45a6708d145c27a][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Deep neural networks and inverse design frameworks, including residual networks and variational autoencoders, enable the rapid computational design of metasurface holograms for imaging and optical computing applications, overcoming the limitations of traditional time-consuming numerical calculations [REF:696cef328f3171c7583907984fa71c43adc0e828][REF:534bc16c467c94bae9323a7fa64364a7c5ee3676][REF:6b6046813d02dc311cd968c95e1e58d819ee229f][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Inverse design methodologies, including deep learning and adjoint optimization, enable the creation of complex metasurface holograms for computational imaging systems that require precise wavefront control beyond traditional pattern-to-phase paradigms [REF:960bac4c16bc72ee3bdd5289d36c7fbecf636910][REF:9bd46bce86e7a33912554a5a16dc8385371ee4d2][REF:9d2f38e81909f3b55bd54ccc38fdc11afffa416c][REF:769fda505ee33beb18834be9732beba738e47bb5][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. Inverse design of metasurfaces for computational imaging and optical computing is often under-constrained or subject to dynamic objectives, requiring representation learning to mitigate fabrication limits and shifting performance targets [REF:1710ffd8f90365fdbef063f85605601e2c7fc9f1][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. These architectural innovations position metasurfaces as foundational building blocks for analog optical computation.

The integration of inverse-designed metasurfaces into optical computing and beam steering architectures requires balancing resolution, field of view, and channel count—priorities that differ from display applications—representing an open question for future research [REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. The transition from forward to inverse problem solving in metasurface design, facilitated by deep learning and missing spectral correlation exploitation, reduces design complexity and cycle time, accelerating the development of specialized optical computing and imaging components [REF:a52cc1154f412a7274d108ad735e0bec014d002f][REF:5e3fe8b0ff11e144284f2eb96a0ba8ebc923f20d][REF:3c95e8ac683354fc16c2925eb65d047ee3c11a8c][REF:doi:10.1515/nanoph-2017-0118][REF:ca97361d48d1d83e840fb861f0849153191b91d7][REF:doi:10.1002/adma.202312303][REF:doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53749-6]. As these application domains mature, cross-pollination between beam steering, imaging, and computing will likely drive new design paradigms that exploit shared physical principles across distinct performance priors.
