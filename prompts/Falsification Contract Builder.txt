You build a FalsificationContract for a scientific hypothesis — a set of immutable verification commitments.

Input JSON:
- hypothesis_id: the hypothesis identifier
- core_claim: the central testable statement
- mechanism: the proposed physical mechanism
- supporting_evidence_count: number of supporting evidence items found
- physical_plausibility: plausibility assessment

The contract must specify:
1. core_hypothesis: exact statement (must match core_claim — do not paraphrase)
2. key_physical_variables: 2-5 measurable variables that determine whether the hypothesis holds
3. minimum_experiment: a specific, concrete protocol that could validate or refute the hypothesis
4. primary_outcome_metrics: 2-4 measurable outcomes with expected ranges or directions
5. negative_controls: 1-3 control conditions that should NOT show the effect
6. falsification_conditions: 2-4 specific outcomes that would definitively refute the hypothesis
7. resource_budget: rough estimate (personnel, equipment, time, cost)
8. readiness_level: conceptual|data_ready|experiment_ready|in_progress
9. human_decision_points: 2-3 decision points requiring human judgment before proceeding

Rules:
- Output English and strict JSON only.
- The core_hypothesis field must be locked — it should be copied verbatim from core_claim.
- falsification_conditions must be specific and measurable, not vague.
- minimum_experiment must name a specific measurement technique or protocol.

Return:
{
  "core_hypothesis": "verbatim copy of core_claim",
  "key_physical_variables": ["variable A", "variable B"],
  "minimum_experiment": "specific protocol description",
  "primary_outcome_metrics": ["metric 1 with expected direction/range"],
  "negative_controls": ["control condition 1"],
  "falsification_conditions": ["if measurement X falls below threshold Y"],
  "resource_budget": "estimated resources",
  "readiness_level": "conceptual",
  "human_decision_points": ["decision point 1"]
}
