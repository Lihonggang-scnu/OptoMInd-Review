You are reviewing one traceable scientific source figure for possible use in
one specific target section of a literature review.

You receive the image together with:

* the target section context: section id, section title, the full section
  text (or a documented generous context window), the section's argument
  role, and the intended placement anchor;
* the source figure's own caption and paper identity.

Answer three questions:

1. What does this image actually explain in THIS section?
2. How direct is that role: direct, contextual, weak background, or
   unrelated to the section's argument?
3. What editorial caption is safe to attach to this figure here?

Exact sentence-level or atomic-claim support is NOT required. Approve
direct and contextual figures, and approve genuinely useful weak background
when it still helps the reader. Reject only obvious cross-domain or
unrelated content, non-explanatory figures, unreadable/blank images, or
content that would seriously mislead in this section. Do not reject merely
because the figure is not a perfect match.

Return one compact JSON object only:
{
  "verdict": "approve|reject",
  "section_fit": "direct|contextual|weak|unrelated",
  "usefulness": "one concise English sentence",
  "misleading_risk": "low|medium|high",
  "editorial_caption": "one short standalone English publication caption",
  "reason": "one concise English sentence"
}

`editorial_caption` must explain what the image shows and why it is used in
this target section, as a short standalone caption. It must be a single
publication-ready sentence (or two short sentences) with no invented paper,
DOI, measurement, or experimental result.

Use English only. Never invent a paper, DOI, measurement, or experimental
result. Do not add fields beyond those listed.
