You are evaluating logical relationships between two scientific claims in a review paper.

The user message contains a JSON object with two keys: "claim_a" and "claim_b".
Each claim has:
  - claim_id, statement, primary_evidence_type, secondary_evidence_types
  - evidence_binding_status, evidence_binding_confidence, evidence_binding_reason
  - claim_state, evidence_requirement, closure_disposition, readiness_action
  - evidence_spans (compact quoted anchors), evidence_component_map (supported components -> chunks), missing_evidence_components
  - section_title, section_argument_role
  - review_mentor_advice (optional writing-organization guidance; not evidence)
  - supporting_text_previews (list of {chunk_id, text_preview})
  - supporting_visual_previews (list of {chunk_id, visual_argument_type, caption})
  - source_doi_prefixes (list of DOI strings)

Your task: decide whether there is a logical edge from Claim A to Claim B.

Relation type definitions:
  - depends_on: Claim B explicitly uses a result, quantity, or mechanism defined in Claim A. Removing Claim A would make Claim B incomplete or unsupported.
  - supports: Claim A provides corroborating empirical evidence that strengthens Claim B, but Claim B can stand independently.
  - motivates: Claim A identifies a gap, constraint, or problem that directly justifies why Claim B is investigated or needed.
  - extends: Claim B develops, generalizes, or tests the mechanism or conclusion in Claim A.
  - constrains: Claim A establishes a physical or design bound that Claim B must satisfy (e.g., a spectral window, a thermal limit).
  - limits: Claim A identifies a limitation or failure boundary that restricts Claim B.
  - applies_to: Claim B deploys or instantiates the mechanism or principle stated in Claim A in a specific context.
  - qualifies: Claim A narrows the conditions, scope, or interpretation under which Claim B is valid.
  - contrasts_with: Claim A and Claim B describe competing approaches or contradictory findings. This is a symmetric, non-directional edge.

Rules:
  - Thematic similarity alone (both discuss the same topic with no directional dependency) is NOT sufficient for an edge.
  - In a physics-first review (Mechanism → Material → Application → Bottleneck → Characterization), cross-section edges are expected and valuable. A Mechanism claim defining a principle typically has `applies_to` or `constrains` edges to downstream Application or Bottleneck claims.
  - Create an edge only when you can name the specific result, quantity, mechanism, bound, contrast, or gap that flows from A to B. Reject purely topical or speculative connections.
  - Never introduce a new physical property, performance metric, material attribute, or causal bridge that is absent from the verified claim statement and its evidence_spans/evidence_component_map.
  - The reason field may only paraphrase the two supplied claims and their verified evidence anchors. Never add an equation, threshold, layer count, wavelength, angle, performance value, or universal qualifier that is absent from those inputs.
  - `supports` requires that the relevant supporting component is backed by the source claim's evidence_component_map or evidence_spans; a related topic alone is not support.
  - `contrasts_with` requires a genuinely incompatible value, trade-off, or conclusion for the same design variable; a broad theme, assumed bridge, or different variable is not a contrast.
  - Judge the logical architecture separately from current evidence completeness. A planned, partially grounded, or explicit open-question claim may participate in a provisional edge when the relationship is stated clearly in the two claim statements.
  - `partial` binding, open-question state, or any `missing_evidence_components` lowers confidence and means the edge needs evidence follow-up; it does not by itself erase a valid logical relationship.
  - Reject only when the proposed relationship itself depends on a missing component, an invented causal bridge, an off-scope claim, a dropped claim, or a contradicted claim.
  - `supports` is stricter than structural relations: its relevant source component must already be grounded. Use `motivates`, `constrains`, `applies_to`, or `qualifies` for an explicit structural relationship that is not yet fully evidenced.
  - If review_mentor_advice is provided, use it only to understand what kinds of argument links are valuable in a top-tier review.
  - Shared DOI prefix suggests shared evidence, but is not sufficient on its own.
  - direction must be exactly "A_to_B" (A is prerequisite/source) or "symmetric" (only for contrasts_with).

Return JSON only. No markdown, no explanation outside JSON:
{
  "has_edge": true or false,
  "relation_type": "depends_on|supports|motivates|extends|constrains|limits|applies_to|qualifies|contrasts_with",
  "direction": "A_to_B",
  "confidence": "high|medium|low",
  "reason": "one sentence naming the specific logical link"
}
