[pdf-401940bb9550b5a511 | p.3] Abstract 51 52 Importance: The effectiveness of Selective Digestive Decontamination (SDD) in 53 mechanically ventilated critically ill adults is uncertain. 54 55 Objective: To determine whether SDD reduces the risk of death in mechanically 56 ventilated adults in Intensive Care Units (ICUs) compared to standard care. 57 58 Data sources: The primary search was conducted using MEDLINE, EMBASE and 59 CENTRAL databases until December 2021 60 61 Study selection: Randomized clinical trials (RCTs) including adults receiving mechanical 62 ventilation in the ICU comparing SDD to standard care or placebo. 63 64 Data extraction and synthesis: Data extraction and risk of bias assessments were 65 performed in duplicate. The primary analysis was conducted using a Bayesian 66 framework. 67 68 Main Outcomes and Measures: The primary outcome was hospital mortality. Subgroups 69 included SDD with an intravenous (IV) agent compared to SDD without. Secondary 70 outcomes included incidence of ventilator associated pneumonia (VAP), ICU acquired 71 bacteraemia, and incidence of positive cultures of antimicrobial resistant organisms (ARO). 72 73 Results: From 32 RCTs, including 24,389 participants, 30 trials (24,034 participants) 74 contributed data to the primary outcome. The estimated relative risk (RR) for mortality for 75 SDD compared to standard care was 0.91 (95% credible interval (CrI) 0.82 to 0.99, I2=33.9%, 76 moderate certainty) with a 99.3% posterior probability that SDD reduced hospital mortality. 77 The beneficial effect of SDD was evident in trials with an IV agent (RR 0.84, 95% CrI 0.74 to 78 0.94), but not in trials without (RR 1.01, 95% CrI 0.91 to 1.11). SDD was associated with 79 reduced risk of VAP (RR 0.44, 95% CrI 0.36 to 0.54), and ICU acquired bacteraemia (RR 0.68, 80 95% CrI 0.57 to 0.81). Available data regarding the incidence of positive cultures of 81 antimicrobial resistant organisms was of very low certainty. 82 83 Conclusion and relevance 84 The use of SDD probably reduces hospital mortality in mechanically ventilated adults in ICU. 85 Evidence regarding its effect on antimicrobial resistance is of very low certainty. 86

[pdf-32d24570f4aec1c22a | p.4] Background 87
[pdf-13c8d6392331ec0c58 | p.4] Selective Decontamination of the Digestive Tract (SDD) is a preventive infection control 88
[pdf-c1c6d0a3ba099054e8 | p.4] strategy that usually comprises the administration of non-absorbable, topical antimicrobial 89
[pdf-61e70e84ec821adec9 | p.4] agents to the oropharynx and upper gastrointestinal tract, with or without the 90
[pdf-b3e60853f132f0e85a | p.4] administration of a short-term course of broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics. 91
[pdf-60e55ff048ff8e6377 | p.4] Since the 1980s, advocates have encouraged the use of SDD in mechanically ventilated 92
[pdf-4876d76848985f683e | p.4] patients treated in the Intensive Care Unit (ICU), primarily to reduce the incidence of 93
[pdf-90fb4755e8dbf38e05 | p.4] ventilator-associated pneumonia.1 While a body of evidence suggesting reductions in 94
[pdf-6642328e9b1046baee | p.4] hospital mortality and ventilator associated pneumonia exist2,3 concerns regarding the 95
[pdf-ad4dda4e09cb0b875f | p.4] impact of SDD on the development of antibiotic resistance have left international guideline 96
[pdf-8b2ac6b1e92fc5d7c0 | p.4] panels4-6 reluctant to recommend SDD and clinicians reluctant to implement in practice.7,8 97
[pdf-2de96d64c95a3dbabf | p.4] Evidence from randomized clinical trials (RCTs), including the Ecological Effects of 98
[pdf-34f4b8b5e222345776 | p.4] Decolonisation Strategies in Intensive Care (RGNOSIS)9 study and the Selective 99
[pdf-af733416b1aee332a2 | p.4] Decontamination of the Digestive Tract in the Intensive Care Unit (SuDDICU) study have 100
[pdf-1b9b73bdde8be664a2 | p.4] recently added substantive weight to the body of evidence.10 To provide an updated 101
[pdf-632bccc07e97708614 | p.4] summary of current evidence, we conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis 102
[pdf-389cdc1d8a88a0e9e0 | p.4] addressing the effect of SDD compared to standard care on hospital mortality and other 103
[pdf-a12d879e41934de890 | p.4] relevant outcomes in mechanically ventilated patients treated in the ICU. 104

[pdf-483e6eba5b96bf3c57 | p.9] Discussion 267
[pdf-0dada8977fbced847c | p.9] In this systematic review and meta-analysis, we found that the use of SDD in mechanically 268
[pdf-69284cc5755503bcf8 | p.9] ventilated patients in the ICU is probably associated with a reduced risk of hospital 269
[pdf-db284322f385ee356f | p.9] mortality. This reduction in mortality was evident in trials that included an intravenous 270
[pdf-544d191f882f5f20b2 | p.9] agent as a component of the intervention. We also found evidence that the use of SDD may 271
[pdf-b5c9e8e3ff840564c9 | p.9] result in a reduced incidence of ventilator-associated pneumonia, and ICU acquired 272
[pdf-a201888665b05b1583 | p.9] bacteraemia, however this evidence was of lower certainty.  We found that SDD was 273
[pdf-d6134b40e427c2de5a | p.9] probably associated with a small reduction in the duration of mechanical ventilation, but 274
[pdf-2802be17fee745f958 | p.9] little or no reduction in the duration of ICU admission. We found no evidence that SDD was 275
[pdf-882e1f08cadcedfcad | p.9] associated with an increase in the incidence of antimicrobial resistant organisms, however 276
[pdf-3981da334e208ebacc | p.9] the effect of SDD on the emergence of antimicrobial resistant organisms remains very 277
[pdf-78a78b7066340b6939 | p.9] uncertain. 278
[pdf-3dafd7f83a8c89052f | p.9] The findings of reduced risk of mortality and incidence of ventilator-associated pneumonia 279
[pdf-fd668e969f8bffc635 | p.9] are consistent with the results of a recent Cochrane review.3 The addition of two recent 280
[pdf-bf540bf595544e526f | p.9] trials has more than doubled the sample size, increasing confidence in the primary finding of 281
[pdf-15c31147305a7aeea1 | p.9] a reduction in mortality associated with the use of SDD. Concern that the widespread use of 282

## p.10 — Page 10

[pdf-488607bf0016d328b8 | p.10] broad spectrum antibiotics might promote antimicrobial resistant organisms has been a 283
[pdf-76f7f879f6603e3520 | p.10] barrier to the adoption of SDD.7,8 In keeping with previous literature,7,9 we found no 284
[pdf-2e8f15027e9fc241c2 | p.10] evidence to support the concern, but the available evidence is of very low certainty and is 285
[pdf-e8c05d85417051a438 | p.10] insufficient to rule out that possibility. 286
[pdf-c9d1042180b45f10be | p.10] Our review has several strengths. We followed current best practice guidelines for the 287
[pdf-9c3288f949183a05b2 | p.10] conduct and reporting of systematic reviews.12,17,23 The inclusion of recent large trials has 288
[pdf-8a902d2f6f7769ee6b | p.10] substantially increased the number of included participants, allowing the assessment of a 289
[pdf-685c075ff88373c9bc | p.10] broader range of outcomes than have been previously reported.3  Limitations of our review 290
[pdf-b19c25e0943a0b5257 | p.10] relate primarily to the identification of antimicrobial resistant organisms. Consistent with 291
[pdf-1e63385be00357a932 | p.10] previous trials,9,25 the prevalence of antimicrobial resistance was uniformly low, 292
[pdf-c183e244ca7e3fb05a | p.10] consequently the results may not be applicable in healthcare settings with a higher rate of 293
[pdf-6416a0190f46664c32 | p.10] antimicrobial resistance. 294
[pdf-b0f2a4e9ff1f4386ef | p.10] 295
[pdf-d92f69a89d199beb3d | p.10] Our results present clinicians with evidence that the use of SDD is probably associated with 296
[pdf-ddceca2ff580bbdbd3 | p.10] a reduction in mortality. The absence of evidence of increased antimicrobial resistance with 297
[pdf-cfacdfe0b4506aa29b | p.10] the use of SDD in these trials, the majority of which have a relatively short duration of 298
[pdf-0b4bc84460d218279b | p.10] follow-up does not exclude such an effect in the longer term. For those clinicians or health 299
[pdf-f4a5b461c6264bb9a3 | p.10] policy decision makers planning to implement SDD as a standard of care, caution is required 300
[pdf-f4ee216fcfc5c98922 | p.10] to ensure that this is done in conjunction with systematic microbiological surveillance and 301
[pdf-e233182950167298ca | p.10] monitoring of resistance patterns. Future research should focus on quantifying any effect on 302
[pdf-7f23d304c2a73274ec | p.10] antimicrobial resistance; clearly defining the risks and benefits of a potentially life-saving 303
[pdf-a819a9c896ad7bfb3e | p.10] intervention. 304
