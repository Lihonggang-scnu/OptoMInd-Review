# Gut Microbiome Modulation of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Efficacy in Solid Tumours: Mechanisms and Clinical Evidence

**研究问题：** How does the gut microbiome modulate the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors in solid tumours, and what mechanisms mediate that effect?
**状态：** partial
**规划阶段：** level1

## 综述范围与论证主线

### 规划内容
  - **综述标题:** Gut Microbiome Modulation of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Efficacy in Solid Tumours: Mechanisms and Clinical Evidence
  - **中心问题:** How does the gut microbiome modulate the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors in solid tumours, and what mechanisms mediate that effect?
  - **Provisional scope:** This review synthesizes evidence on the role of the gut microbiome in modulating immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) efficacy across solid tumours. It covers clinical associations (antibiotic exposure timing, FMT outcomes), microbial signatures (taxa, metabolites), mechanistic pathways (immune modulation, metabolic signaling, barrier integrity), and therapeutic interventions. The scope excludes hematological malignancies unless mechanistic insights are directly transferable to solid tumours. Emphasis is placed on human clinical data over preclinical models, with explicit notation of evidence grades (observational vs. interventional).
#### Scope adjustments from supplements
  - Explicitly state that no completed Phase III randomized controlled trials of microbiome interventions in ICI-treated solid tumour patients exist as of late 2025; current evidence relies on Phase I/II trials and observational data.
  - Acknowledge that human tumour microenvironment metabolite concentrations (inosine, SCFAs) are not directly quantified in available studies; plasma stratification data and proxy biomarkers (UBA6 expression) are used instead.
  - Distinguish tumor-type specificity in antibiotic sensitivity (head/neck, RCC, breast show stronger post-ICI antibiotic effects; lung, melanoma show weaker associations in large cohort data).
  - Note that FMT Phase II data shows promising ORR (70-80% in NSCLC/melanoma) but elevated irAE rates in dual-ICI contexts (65% grade ≥3 AEs in melanoma cohort).

## 共同提纲

### 规划内容
#### Chapters
  - **Clinical Landscape: Antibiotics, FMT, and Observational Data**
    - **本节作用:** Evaluate the strength of clinical evidence linking microbiome perturbations to ICI outcomes, distinguishing observational associations from interventional data.
    - **Question:** What is the current state of clinical evidence regarding microbiome modulation of ICI efficacy, and what are the evidence grade limitations?
##### Evidence basis
    - Large-scale observational data on antibiotic timing (21,108 patients; post-ICI exposure HR 1.27 for mortality)
    - Phase II FMT-LUMINate trial outcomes (ORR 80% NSCLC, 75% melanoma; 65% grade ≥3 AEs in dual-ICI arm)
    - Explicit absence of Phase III RCTs confirmed through multiple supplement searches
    - **Scope change from supplement:** Added explicit statement on Phase III RCT absence; refined antibiotic timing claims to distinguish pre- vs. post-ICI windows with tumor-type stratification.
  - **Microbial Signatures: Taxa and Metabolites**
    - **本节作用:** Identify specific microbial features associated with response or resistance, noting quantification limitations in human samples.
    - **Question:** Which specific taxa and metabolites predict ICI response in solid tumours, and what are the measurement constraints?
##### Evidence basis
    - Plasma inosine stratification correlating with OS in RCC (median OS 33 vs. 22 months high vs. low)
    - SCFA fecal levels associated with PFS in prospective cohorts (52 patients)
    - No absolute human TME concentration data available; reliance on stratification and proxy biomarkers
    - **Scope change from supplement:** Added explicit limitation on metabolite quantification; cannot report μM/ng/mL values for human TME inosine or SCFAs.
  - **Mechanistic Pathways: Immune, Metabolic, and Barrier Functions**
    - **本节作用:** Elucidate the biological mechanisms mediating microbiome-ICI interactions, distinguishing preclinical mechanisms from human validation.
    - **Question:** What molecular and cellular mechanisms mediate the effect of the microbiome on ICI efficacy, and which have human validation?
##### Evidence basis
    - Inosine-UBA6 inhibition pathway enhancing MHC-I expression (mouse models; human UBA6 protein expression correlation)
    - SCFA-mediated HDAC inhibition and GPR signaling (preclinical; limited human mechanistic data)
    - Gut barrier integrity markers (sMAdCAM-1) correlating with PFS in Phase III JAVELIN Renal 101 biomarker analysis
    - **Scope change from supplement:** Clarified that UBA6 mechanism has human protein expression support but no direct human metabolite concentration validation.
  - **Therapeutic Interventions: FMT, Probiotics, and Diet**
    - **本节作用:** Assess the feasibility and efficacy of microbiome-targeted interventions, noting trial phase limitations.
    - **Question:** Can microbiome interventions reliably enhance ICI efficacy in clinical practice, and what is the current trial phase status?
##### Evidence basis
    - FMT Phase II trials (FMT-LUMINate: ORR 70-80%; safety signals vary by ICI regimen)
    - Engineered bacteria Phase I data (SYNB1891, MRx0518 safety signals)
    - No Phase III efficacy data; regulatory challenges for Live Biotherapeutic Products
    - **Scope change from supplement:** Explicitly framed as Phase I/II evidence only; cannot claim Phase III validation exists.
  - **Limitations, Heterogeneity, and Future Directions**
    - **本节作用:** Address evidence gaps, methodological challenges, and research priorities based on confirmed absence of Phase III data and metabolite quantification.
    - **Question:** What are the major limitations in current research and what is needed for clinical translation?
##### Evidence basis
    - Confirmed absence of Phase III RCTs for microbiome interventions in ICI-treated solid tumours
    - Lack of standardized metabolite quantification in human TME
    - Tumor-type and ICI-regimen specificity in intervention outcomes
    - Regulatory and standardization barriers for microbiome therapeutics
    - **Scope change from supplement:** Elevated Phase III absence from 'search gap' to 'confirmed field limitation'; added metabolite quantification as priority research need.
